\section{Evidence Lifetime Across Model Upgrades}\label{sec:gaps}

A central objection to empirical AI safety evaluations on small open-weight checkpoints is that behavioral measurements on \(4\mathrm{B}\) or \(8\mathrm{B}\) models need not transfer to stronger frontier systems. That objection is decisive for endogenous alignment claims and exact for our architecture: \emph{evidence has a well-defined upgrade lifetime}. Any quantity conditioned on a specific model's action distribution expires the moment weights, prompts, or scaffolding change. Conversely, any invariant proved over all syntactically representable inputs and enforced outside the model's address space holds for an arbitrarily capable proposer.

\subsection{Prior Work and Architectural Gap}

\para{Reference monitors and object capabilities.} Complete mediation, least privilege, and tamperproof reference monitors originate in classic capability and operating-system security~\cite{dennis,saltzer,schneider}, extended to cryptographic caveat attenuation via Macaroons~\cite{birgisson2014}. Machine-checked kernels and systems verifiers such as seL4~\cite{sel4} and Verus~\cite{verus} prove functional invariants over executable state machines, while lease fencing tokens~\cite{kleppmann2016}, write-ahead-logged transactions~\cite{sqlite-wal}, and distributed budget protocols~\cite{ftbudget,limbo} prevent split-brain or duplicate writes. Current agent frameworks~\cite{controlroom} track or halt compute workloads centrally, yet rarely carry cryptographic epoch fences all the way to external tool sinks.

\para{AI control, prompt-injection defense, and shutdown.} AI control protocols~\cite{control,terekhov,makins,metraudit} evaluate untrusted models under trusted monitoring, while benchmarks and privilege-control systems such as AgentDojo~\cite{agentdojo}, CaMeL~\cite{camel}, Progent~\cite{progent}, Guaranteed Safe AI~\cite{gsai}, and containment verification~\cite{moon} restrict tool arguments, isolate data flows, or gate RL policies~\cite{shield,cpo}. Static per-call schemas such as Progent block direct argument injections effectively, yet stateless checks cannot enforce cumulative shared budgets across concurrent sub-agents or revoke work already dispatched to a sink. Similarly, classic instrumental-convergence and off-switch analyses~\cite{omohundro2008,soares2015,offswitch} and modern anti-scheming evaluations~\cite{safetycase,needham,schoen,korbak} study when an agent \emph{chooses} to allow interruption; we enforce interruption exogenously at the sink regardless of agent preferences.
