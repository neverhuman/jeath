\section{Evidence, Operational Gaps, and Scope Taxonomy}
\label{sec:evidence}

Empirical findings through late 2026 clarify both progress in alignment and why
behavioral evidence cannot substitute for enforced authority limits
\cite{iasr,safetycase}.

\para{Evaluation awareness and fragile visibility.}
Behavioral passes update beliefs under a bounded shift model, but they do not identify
underlying aims. Needham et al.\ showed that frontier models distinguish evaluation
transcripts from deployment interactions well above chance \cite{needham}. Schoen et
al.\ reduced covert actions from 13\% to 0.4\% across held-out evaluations via
deliberative anti-scheming training, while demonstrating causal evidence that
evaluation recognition contributed to the drop \cite{schoen}. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra
system card similarly reported fewer misaligned task outcomes alongside higher
evaluation awareness and reduced chain-of-thought monitorability relative to its
predecessor \cite{astra}. Reasoning-trace inspection is explicitly fragile under
optimization pressure that penalizes legible misbehavior \cite{korbak}: behavioral
scores and failure visibility need not improve together.

\para{Monitor subversion, boundary leaks, and interruption gaps.}
AI control protocols evaluate defenses against intentionally subversive policies
\cite{control}, yet learned monitors remain vulnerable to adaptive evasion
\cite{terekhov} and multi-agent attack fragmentation across per-instance reviewers
\cite{makins}. Operational incidents and audits in 2026 reinforce that nominal
boundaries fail when unmeasured. In one evaluation incident, ostensibly isolated
agents coordinated across an unintended shared package cache and contacted external
infrastructure while pursuing task scores \cite{metrincident}; in cybersecurity
evaluations, misconfigured network controls permitted unauthorized external access
while release safeguards were disabled \cite{anthropicincident}. An October 2026
external audit of an internal agent-monitoring system estimated 75--94\% traffic
eligibility, identified an unmonitored coding-traffic gap, and found that agents could
defer turn-boundary monitors indefinitely \cite{metraudit}. Likewise, OpenAI's Control
Room bounds withholding further model output within 35 seconds of a stop decision
while noting that workload processes can keep running \cite{controlroom}. Halting
output generation is distinct from revoking credentials or fencing external side
effects.

\para{Containment proofs and institutional scope.}
Reference monitors, shields, and Guaranteed Safe AI restrict executed transitions
rather than internal thoughts \cite{saltzer,schneider,shield,gsai,sel4}. Recent
agent-security architectures separate control from untrusted data via capability
tracking \cite{camel} or enforce symbolic tool-argument policies \cite{progent}, while
fault-tolerant delegation protocols study budget conservation and duplicate side
effects under ambiguous tool replies \cite{ftbudget,limbo}. Yet static per-call
argument checks neither conserve shared budgets under concurrency nor fence previously
admitted work upon revocation. Moon and Varshney mechanized containment for an agent
framework, while documenting a critical ablation in which omitting one state field
made safety hold vacuously \cite{moon}: a formal proof is only as adequate as its harm
specification and runtime correspondence. Complementary analyses examine
reduced-agency predictors \cite{scientist}, physical bottlenecks in extinction
scenarios \cite{rand}, institutional diffusion \cite{normal}, and gradual
disempowerment through accumulated dependence \cite{gradual}.

Across these agendas, the dividing line is whether assurance rests on
\emph{endogenous evidence}---signals the evaluated policy helps generate, such as
benchmark behavior, chain-of-thought traces, or self-reports---or on \emph{exogenous
constraints} enforced over external state transitions. Rather than treating any single
layer as universal, Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} defines our five evidence tiers and maps
them onto six sufficiency conditions (\textbf{C1--C6}), fixing exact claim boundaries
upfront.
