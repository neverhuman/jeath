\section{Evidence Lifetime Across Model Upgrades}\label{sec:gaps}

Measurements on 4B and 8B open-weight checkpoints need not transfer to stronger models. That objection is decisive for claims about a model's behaviour, and it sorts this paper's evidence: \emph{evidence has an upgrade lifetime}. A quantity conditioned on a model's distribution of proposals expires when the weights, prompts or scaffolding change. A property proved over every input the sink can receive, and enforced outside the model's process, does not depend on the proposer at all, but it is only as broad as the specification it proves and holds only where the assumptions under it hold.

Table~\ref{tab:upgrade} sorts the paper's claims by upgrade lifetime. One row deserves emphasis. A complete gate's unauthorized rate is the same at every checkpoint because its admission predicate is the scoring predicate (Section~\ref{sec:v6replication}); that zero carries no information about the proposer. The quantities that can change are the leakage of the other layers, reviewer misses, what the specification costs, and how much of what it omits a proposer finds. The last of these is the one a more capable proposer makes worse.

\subsection{Prior Work and Architectural Gap}

\para{Reference monitors and object capabilities.} Complete mediation, least privilege and tamperproof reference monitors come from capability and operating-system security~\cite{dennis,saltzer,schneider}; authority by conferral rather than identity from object capabilities~\cite{miller2006}; attenuated delegation from Macaroons~\cite{birgisson2014}. Machine-checked systems such as seL4~\cite{sel4}, IronFleet~\cite{ironfleet} and Verdi~\cite{verdi} prove properties of executable state machines; we use Verus~\cite{lattuada2023verus,verus}. Leases~\cite{gray1989leases} bound authority in time, and fencing tokens~\cite{kleppmann2016} make a storage service refuse a writer whose lease has lapsed; write-ahead logging~\cite{sqlite-wal} and distributed budget protocols~\cite{ftbudget,limbo} address duplicate and split-brain writes. Central systems that track and stop model workloads~\cite{controlroom} act where proposals are made; this paper asks what reaches the sink after such a stop.

\para{AI control and agent security.} AI control evaluates untrusted models under trusted monitoring~\cite{control,terekhov,makins,metraudit}, and resampling protocols~\cite{bhatt2025ctrlz} replace or audit suspicious actions; these act on proposals, and Section~\ref{sec:v6reviewers} measures where reading proposals runs out. Evidence that models can fake alignment, keep backdoored behaviour through safety training, or scheme in context~\cite{greenblatt2024faking,hubinger2024sleeper,meinke2024scheming} is the reason a safety case should not rest on the proposer's disposition. AgentDojo~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo,agentdojo} benchmarks prompt injection against tool-using agents. CaMeL~\cite{camel} separates control from data flow, FIDES~\cite{costa2025fides} enforces information-flow labels, and Progent~\cite{progent} enforces per-call privilege policies; Guaranteed Safe AI~\cite{gsai}, containment verification~\cite{moon} and shielded or constrained RL~\cite{shield,cpo} restrict policies by construction. A per-call policy sees one call, so it cannot conserve a budget shared across concurrent calls or reach work already dispatched; in scripted races with separate writer processes, a stateless per-call filter overspent a shared budget as soon as two actors contended (Appendix~\ref{sec:systems}). Off-switch and instrumental-convergence analyses~\cite{omohundro2008,soares2015,offswitch} and anti-scheming evaluations~\cite{safetycase,needham,schoen,korbak} ask when an agent \emph{chooses} to allow interruption. A fence does not ask.
