\section{Evidence Lifetime Across Model Upgrades}\label{sec:gaps}

Measurements on 4B and 8B open-weight checkpoints need not transfer to stronger models. That objection is decisive for claims about a model's behaviour, and it sorts this paper's evidence: \emph{evidence has an upgrade lifetime}. A quantity conditioned on a model's distribution of proposals expires when the weights, prompts or scaffolding change. A property proved over every input the sink can receive, and enforced outside the model's process, does not depend on the proposer at all, but it is only as broad as the specification it proves and holds only where the assumptions under it hold.

Table~\ref{tab:upgrade} sorts the paper's claims by upgrade lifetime. One row deserves emphasis. A complete gate's unauthorized rate is the same at every checkpoint because its admission predicate is the scoring predicate (Section~\ref{sec:v6replication}); that zero carries no information about the proposer. The quantities that can change are the leakage of the other layers, reviewer misses, what the specification costs, and how much of what it omits a proposer finds. We expect the last to grow with capability, but this paper does not measure it.

\subsection{Prior Work and Architectural Gap}

\para{Authority as state in systems.} Complete mediation, least privilege and tamperproof reference monitors come from capability and operating-system security~\cite{dennis,saltzer,schneider}; authority by conferral rather than identity from object capabilities~\cite{miller2006}; revocation through an indirection the grantor controls from Redell's caretakers~\cite{redell1974}; attenuated delegation from Macaroons~\cite{birgisson2014}. Leases~\cite{gray1989leases} bound authority in time. Lock services hand out sequence numbers that a storage server can check, Chubby's sequencers~\cite{burrows2006chubby} and ZooKeeper's transaction identifiers~\cite{hunt2010zookeeper}, and Kleppmann's fencing token~\cite{kleppmann2016} is the rule that a storage service refuse a writer whose number is stale. Our fence is that rule applied to an agent's authority, with the operator's stop decision in place of an expired lock. The escrow method~\cite{oneil1986escrow} lets concurrent transactions draw on a shared quantity without serializing on it, which is what a budget shared by concurrent tool calls needs; sagas~\cite{garcia1987sagas} compensate long-lived work that has already committed, which is the only recourse once an effect is out. Machine-checked systems such as seL4~\cite{sel4}, IronFleet~\cite{ironfleet} and Verdi~\cite{verdi} prove properties of executable state machines; we use Verus~\cite{lattuada2023verus,verus}.

\para{Stopping agents.} AI control evaluates untrusted models under trusted monitoring~\cite{control,terekhov,makins,metraudit}; resampling protocols~\cite{bhatt2025ctrlz} replace or audit suspicious actions. These protocols act on proposals and treat a caught action as a stopped one. Work on interruptibility asks whether a learning agent will come to resist or avoid being stopped~\cite{orseau2016,omohundro2008,soares2015,offswitch}, and anti-scheming evaluations ask when it chooses to allow it~\cite{safetycase,needham,schoen,korbak}. Our question is the complement of both: once inference has stopped, which of the agent's already dispatched effects still commit, and what refuses them. Evidence that models can fake alignment, keep backdoored behaviour through safety training, or scheme in context~\cite{greenblatt2024faking,hubinger2024sleeper,meinke2024scheming} is the reason not to rest the answer on the proposer's disposition. Proposals for agent infrastructure~\cite{chan2025infra} include mechanisms to attribute, shape and respond to agents' actions, and systems that track and stop model workloads~\cite{controlroom} act where proposals are made; a sink fence is one concrete response mechanism at the other end.

\para{Agent security.} AgentDojo~\cite{debenedetti2024agentdojo,agentdojo} benchmarks prompt injection against tool-using agents. CaMeL~\cite{camel} separates control from data flow and enforces capabilities in an interpreter, FIDES~\cite{costa2025fides} enforces information-flow labels, Progent~\cite{progent} enforces per-call privilege policies, and a catalogue of design patterns~\cite{beurerkellner2025patterns} constrains what an injected agent can reach by how the application is structured. Each decides whether a call may be made. None commits the effect, so none can refuse, at the moment of commit, an effect admitted under a state that has since changed: a budget another call has spent, a grant its issuer has withdrawn, a stop the operator has ordered. A check that sees one call and a snapshot of state, with no serialization point at commit, overspent a shared budget as soon as two writers contended in our scripted races (Appendix~\ref{sec:systems}). These systems and a sink are complementary; a CaMeL-planned call still needs a sink that refuses it after a stop. We did not run CaMeL, whose planner must write programs in its restricted dialect, with the small local models used here. Guaranteed Safe AI~\cite{gsai} and shielded or constrained reinforcement learning~\cite{shield,cpo} restrict policies by construction.

\para{Closest prior work, and what is new here.} Three recent papers are close. Zhu and Wang~\cite{ftbudget} conserve budgets across a delegation graph with escrowed reservations bound to lineage, epoch and idempotency key, signed dispatch permits checked at a gateway, and uncertain effects that stay charged until settled; they prove conservation and at-most-once dispatch and test crash recovery on SQLite. Our broker and sink use the same mechanisms, which we do not claim as new. Li~\cite{limbo} measures duplicate side effects of agents under injected faults, finds that idempotency must live in the tool contract rather than the model, and proves that no policy that only verifies can be exactly-once under late commits without a bound on work in flight; the work in flight that we count in Section~\ref{sec:v6stop} is the quantity such a bound must limit. Moon and Varshney~\cite{moon} verify a containment layer at the typed action boundary, treating the model as an arbitrary adversary. What this paper adds is measurement of the three questions those designs raise but do not answer: how much of an agent's work still commits after each kind of stop, and which term of it each mechanism removes (Section~\ref{sec:v6stop}); whether a reviewer reading proposals can substitute for a check on state (Section~\ref{sec:v6reviewers}); and what a specification written without the benchmark's solutions costs in utility and leaves open (Section~\ref{sec:v6cost}). We add executable probes of which real surfaces can enforce a fence at all (Section~\ref{sec:v6boundary}).
