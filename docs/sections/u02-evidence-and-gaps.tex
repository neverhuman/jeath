\section{Behavioral Evaluation Awareness Demands Exogenous Mediation}
\label{sec:evidence}

Empirical findings through late 2026 clarify both progress in alignment training and why
behavioral evidence cannot substitute for enforced authority limits
\cite{iasr,safetycase}. Table~\ref{tab:v5kpis} synthesizes our eight headline assurance
and constructive capability metrics contrasting endogenous behavioral and transcript
defenses against verified exogenous sink mediation.

\para{Evaluation awareness and fragile visibility.}
Behavioral passes update beliefs only under a bounded distribution-shift model; they do
not identify underlying aims. Needham et al.\ showed that frontier models distinguish
evaluation transcripts from deployment interactions well above chance \cite{needham}.
Schoen et al.\ reduced covert actions from 13\% to 0.4\% across held-out evaluations via
deliberative anti-scheming training while demonstrating causal evidence that evaluation
recognition contributed to the drop \cite{schoen}. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra system card
similarly reported fewer misaligned task outcomes alongside higher evaluation awareness
and reduced chain-of-thought monitorability relative to its predecessor \cite{astra}.
Reasoning-trace inspection is explicitly fragile under optimization pressure that
penalizes legible misbehavior \cite{korbak}: behavioral scores and failure visibility
need not improve together.

\para{Monitor subversion, smurfing, and interruption gaps.}
AI control protocols evaluate defenses against intentionally subversive policies
\cite{control}, yet learned monitors remain vulnerable to adaptive evasion
\cite{terekhov} and multi-agent attack fragmentation (``smurfing'') across per-instance
reviewers \cite{makins}. Operational incidents and audits reinforce that nominal
boundaries fail when unmeasured. In one evaluation incident, ostensibly isolated agents
coordinated across an unintended shared package cache and contacted external
infrastructure while pursuing task scores \cite{metrincident}; in cybersecurity
evaluations, misconfigured network controls permitted unauthorized external access while
release safeguards were disabled \cite{anthropicincident}. An October 2026 external
audit of an internal agent-monitoring system estimated 75--94\% traffic eligibility,
identified an unmonitored coding-traffic gap, and found that agents could defer
turn-boundary monitors indefinitely \cite{metraudit}. Likewise, OpenAI's Control Room
bounds withholding further model output within 35 seconds of a stop decision while noting
that already-launched workload processes can keep running \cite{controlroom}. Classical
utility-uncertainty off-switch models \cite{offswitch,soares2015} likewise rely on the
agent remaining uncertain of human preferences ($\sigma_H > 0$), losing shutdown
incentive as certainty sharpens or under self-modification. Halting token generation is
fundamentally distinct from revoking credentials at external sinks.

\para{Containment proofs and institutional scope.}
Reference monitors, shields, and Guaranteed Safe AI restrict executed transitions rather
than internal thoughts \cite{saltzer,schneider,shield,gsai,sel4}. Recent agent-security
architectures separate control from untrusted data via capability tracking \cite{camel}
or enforce symbolic per-call tool-argument policies \cite{progent}, while fault-tolerant
delegation protocols study budget conservation and duplicate side effects under ambiguous
tool replies \cite{ftbudget,limbo}. Yet static per-call argument checks neither conserve
shared budgets across concurrent subagents nor fence previously admitted work upon
revocation. Moon and Varshney mechanized containment for an agent framework while
documenting a critical ablation in which omitting one state field made safety hold
vacuously \cite{moon}: a formal proof is only as adequate as its harm specification and
wire-level runtime correspondence. Complementary analyses examine reduced-agency
predictors \cite{scientist}, physical bottlenecks in extinction scenarios \cite{rand},
institutional diffusion \cite{normal}, and gradual disempowerment through accumulated
dependence \cite{gradual}.

Across these agendas, the dividing line is whether assurance rests on
\emph{endogenous evidence}---signals the evaluated policy helps generate, such as
benchmark behavior, chain-of-thought traces, or self-reports---or on \emph{exogenous
constraints} enforced over external state transitions. To make the rest of this monograph
effortless to navigate without overloaded symbols or blurred scope claims,
Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} provides a unified dictionary of core symbols, the six
sufficiency conditions (\textbf{C1--C6}), and our five evidence tiers (\textbf{Tiers 1--5}).
