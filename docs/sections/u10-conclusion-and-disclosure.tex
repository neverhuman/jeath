\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Intelligence lives in proposals and authority lives in state. Holding authority as state at the sink, and checking it
when an effect commits, makes an agent's reach independent of how it proposes: halting a model stops new proposals,
and only a fence stops the effects already in flight. The independence is exactly as wide as the specification. A
gate that reads state has no learned blind spot for an adversary to search out, but it is blind to whatever its
specification does not name, and a more capable proposer finds more of that, not less. Mediation does not remove the
safety problem; it moves it from a model's dispositions, which no one can enumerate, to a written specification, which
anyone can read, test and argue about. The costs move with it: legitimate work a coarse specification refuses, work in
flight a fence discards, and surfaces that cannot refuse a stale epoch at all.

Three questions decide whether this is enough. Which external surfaces can enforce an epoch themselves rather than
trust the writer to send one (Section~\ref{sec:v6boundary})? How fine must a specification be before its cost in
utility is acceptable, and who writes it (Section~\ref{sec:v6cost})? And do people grant more to agents that are more
capable or more agreeable, so that intelligence regains through conferral the reach that mediation removed
(Observation~\ref{obs:conferral})? The first is an engineering survey; the second is a measurement this paper starts;
the third needs a study with people, whose design we state in Appendix~\ref{app:v6conferral}.

\para{Scope.} The SQLite broker and sink mediate only their own database effects. Nothing here is a sandbox, external
exactly-once execution, a formal refinement of a deployed system, a frontier-model result or evidence from human
participants. Live results use quantized open-weight checkpoints (\vsixCheckpointList) on CPU at temperature 0.
