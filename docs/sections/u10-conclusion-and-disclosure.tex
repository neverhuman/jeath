\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Intelligence lives in proposals and authority lives in state. Holding authority as state at the sink, and checking
it when an effect commits, separates stopping a model from stopping its effects. Halting a model stops new proposals;
a fence also stops the effects already in flight; and only faster enforcement shortens the delay before either acts. For AI control this is a matter of accounting as
much as engineering: an evaluation that counts a caught attack as prevented should also count what the stop did not
reach. A
gate that reads state does not depend on a reviewer's prompt or token budget, but it enforces only what its
specification names. We conjecture that this is the general shape of mediation: it moves the safety problem from a
model's dispositions, which no one can enumerate, to a written specification, which anyone can read, test and argue
about. The costs move with it: legitimate work a coarse specification refuses and attacks it admits because they stay
inside it (Section~\ref{sec:v6cost}), work in flight a fence discards (Section~\ref{sec:v6stop}), and surfaces that cannot refuse a stale epoch at all
(Section~\ref{sec:v6boundary}).

Three questions decide whether this is enough. Which external surfaces can enforce an epoch themselves rather than
trust the writer to send one (Section~\ref{sec:v6boundary}), and how much of what deployed agents do ends in a commit
that such a surface can refuse? How fine must a specification be before its cost in
utility is acceptable, and who writes it (Section~\ref{sec:v6cost})? And do people grant more to agents that are more
capable or more agreeable, so that intelligence regains through conferral the reach that mediation removed
(Observation~\ref{obs:conferral})? The first is an engineering survey and a measurement of real agents' tool traces; the second is a measurement this paper starts;
the third needs a study with people, whose design we state in Appendix~\ref{app:v6conferral}.

\para{Scope.} The SQLite broker and sink mediate only their own database effects. Nothing here is a sandbox, external
exactly-once execution, a formal refinement of a deployed system, a frontier-model result or evidence from human
participants. Live results use quantized open-weight checkpoints (\vsixCheckpointList) on CPU, decoding greedily except for E5's
sampled proposals.
