\section{HON country references, fitting, and uncertainty}

The principal tie-adjusted percentile for score $s$ in a country-by-gender reference of size $N$ is
\begin{equation}
q(s)=\frac{\#\{s_i<s\}+\tfrac12\#\{s_i=s\}}{N}.
\end{equation}
The reference covers all eligible adult ages and is fixed across age. Ties share their midrank. The source-wide within-gender reference and pooled-gender reference provide sensitivities. Their numerical percentiles are different variables and are labelled accordingly.

The common-support country set requires at least 100 eligible profiles of each gender in each of 18--29, 30--44 and 45--64. The spline uses natural boundary conditions at 18 and 64 with interior knots 25, 35, 45 and 55. Exact-age aggregate means are fit using their profile counts, preserving the least-squares solution for those grouped outcomes. Predictions at each exact age in 25--29 and 45--49 are averaged equally within the band before the age contrast is formed. Country contrasts then receive equal weights.

Uncertainty in equal-country summaries uses 2,000 country bootstrap replicates with the recorded seed. The included countries are the resampling units. These intervals are conditional on the observed platform profiles and support rule, and do not account for unknown audience selection or national sampling. Between-country quantiles are displayed separately. Country point estimates and sign counts describe heterogeneity, not a collection of multiplicity-corrected significance tests. \section{Formal derivations and rival models}

\subsection{Observer and context averaging}
Let the independent centered components of a balanced observation be $\theta_i,h_i,b_{ij},c_{ik},\epsilon_{ijk}$ with variances $\sigma_\theta^2,\sigma_h^2,\sigma_b^2,\sigma_c^2,\sigma_\epsilon^2$. Averaging across $m$ observers and $k$ contexts gives
\begin{equation}
\bar Y_i=\theta_i+h_i+\frac1m\sum_jb_{ij}+\frac1k\sum_kc_{ik}+\frac1{mk}\sum_{j,k}\epsilon_{ijk}.
\end{equation}
Independence makes the variance the sum of the five component variances, with the last three scaled by $1/m$, $1/k$ and $1/(mk)$. Since $\Cov(\theta_i,\bar Y_i)=\sigma_\theta^2$, squared correlation is the target variance divided by that total variance. This proves the observer/context identity without requiring Gaussian components. Finite variances, common loadings and the independence assumptions remain essential.

\subsection{Shared error and agreement}
Suppose two independently drawn panels share $\theta_i+h_i$ but have independent remaining errors with variance $v$. Their agreement is
\begin{equation}
\Corr(\bar Y_{i1},\bar Y_{i2})=\frac{\sigma_\theta^2+\sigma_h^2}{\sigma_\theta^2+\sigma_h^2+v},
\end{equation}
whereas squared criterion correlation is $\sigma_\theta^2/(\sigma_\theta^2+\sigma_h^2+v)$. They differ even when panels have identical precision. Agreement can approach one while criterion validity remains below one. If observer errors of variance $\sigma_b^2$ have common pairwise correlation $\rho$, the variance of their mean is $\sigma_b^2[\rho+(1-\rho)/m]$, which has a nonzero limit for $\rho>0$. Repeated presentation and shared reputation are plausible sources of common error, not estimates from the present datasets.

\subsection{Information versus useful context}
The measurement identity assumes a stable target independent of the nuisance components. If the desired outcome is relationship-specific, write $\theta_{i,r}$ or an explicit interaction with a relevant context. Averaging a genuine context dependence away can worsen that forecast. A comparison at fixed observation cost must state how cost depends on observers and contexts. The identity alone supplies neither an optimal allocation nor an optimal courtship duration.

\subsection{Capacity, delivery, and institutions}
For any integrable capacity $B$ and delivery share $p$ with finite conditional covariance,
$\E[pB\mid\mathcal I]=\E[p\mid\mathcal I]\E[B\mid\mathcal I]+\Cov(p,B\mid\mathcal I)$ by the definition of covariance. Under the illustrative deterministic delivered benefit $[e+(1-e)p]B$, differentiating gives marginal capacity $e+(1-e)p$ and marginal willingness $(1-e)B$. These derivatives hold other terms fixed. They do not establish equilibrium behavior, institutional welfare, or the elimination of other motives.

\subsection{Breadth and a common component}
If $X_\ell=ag+s_\ell$ with independent specialist components of common variance, averaging $K$ domains preserves $ag$ and reduces specialist variance to $\sigma_s^2/K$. If specialists instead have pairwise correlation $\rho_s$, their contribution becomes $\sigma_s^2[\rho_s+(1-\rho_s)/K]$. Selecting the maximum performance or using unequal weights produces another score. The old breadth result is conditional on the actual scoring and covariance structure; ``more culture'' alone does not identify those conditions.

\subsection{Parenthood decomposition}
Write $p=\Pr(c>0)$, $\mu_P=\E[z\mid c>0]$, $\mu=\E[z]$, and $\bar c_P=\E[c\mid c>0]$. Because $c=0$ among nonparents, $\E[c]=p\bar c_P$ and
\begin{align}
\frac{\Cov(z,c)}{\E[c]}&=\frac{p\E[zc\mid c>0]}{p\bar c_P}-\mu\\
&=(\mu_P-\mu)+\frac{\Cov(z,c\mid c>0)}{\bar c_P}.
\end{align}
This proof extends to consistent weighted expectations. It is undefined when mean child count is zero. Combining cells with different trait scales or weights changes the estimand; components must be combined using the same declared rule. The decomposition identifies an accounting relation rather than a causal episode effect or inherited response.

\section{Ethnography, contribution, and subsidiary claims}

The reveal/produce/allocate framework assigns each example a measurable outcome and a rival. Maternal ethnobotanical knowledge and political influence concern knowledge or household/social processes and child health, without establishing that mate selection mediated the association \citep{mcdade2007,alami2020}. Storytelling studies concern cooperative content and social preference in particular communities \citep{story}. Hunting success includes practice, ecological opportunity and luck \citep{koster}. Neither prestige nor resources alone measures an inherited cognitive trait.

Cross-cultural authority codes are descriptive records with variable denominators, substantial missingness and nonindependent societies. Their categories distinguish consent from consultation, autonomous choice from family approval, and formal divorce rights from the practical ability to leave. They are not evidence that coercion is absent or that community judgment is uniformly accurate.

\input{minds-ethnography.tex}

MHC-associated odor preferences require convergent evidence across odor tasks, actual pairing and reproductive outcomes. The cited synthesis reports mixed or null aggregate evidence under relevant analyses, so a hidden compatibility channel is not assumed \citep{havlicek2020}. Candidate-variant associations in ADHD-related work do not establish a hunter--farmer taxonomy or a general explanation of cognitive diversity; a Rendille study's nutritional null remains relevant contrary evidence \citep{kunkle2025}. These literatures motivate specific tests rather than contributing numerical estimates to the main pathway.

Genetic-lineage ratios describe the histories of particular inherited markers, affected by demography and lineage loss. They cannot count all people who had children, recover the share who were voluntarily chosen, or identify a cognitive preference. Archaeological games and regulated contests can establish the presence of activities at particular sites and times; they do not identify the cognitive selection coefficient attached to participation. Subsidiary hominin and recent brain-size debates constrain universal trajectories without assigning a causal role to assessment.
