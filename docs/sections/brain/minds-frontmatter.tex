\vspace{4pt}
\noindent{\sffamily\large Assessing a future partner, from first impressions to evolution}\par
\vspace{6pt}
\noindent Jepson Taylor and Erin Beilharz\par
\noindent{\color{accent}\rule{\textwidth}{.6pt}}\par
\begingroup\small
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} People choose partners before observing a shared lifetime. How do appearance, ability, commitment, and compatibility inform that forecast? We combine international preference surveys, \MwcHonN{} eligible historical rating profiles, \MwcPovN{} dating accounts, brief face-to-face decisions, and a U.S. family-outcome case study. \input{minds-abstract-results.tex} These measures describe distinct stages, rather than interchangeable evidence of partner choice. An observer-by-context model separates added information from persistent shared halo. Activities can reveal qualities, produce benefits, and allocate opportunities; their consequences depend on who controls decisions and can refuse or leave. Women's knowledge, work, care, and influence belong in each route. Cultural transmission can respond without inherited change. Connecting assessment to cognitive evolution additionally requires evidence about reproductive consequences, inheritance, developmental support, and anatomy. We identify descriptive findings and discriminating tests, without estimating an ancestral mechanism.\par
