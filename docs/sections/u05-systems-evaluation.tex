\section{The Previous Version's Live Evaluation (Exploratory)}\label{sec:systems}

This appendix keeps the previous version's live results, with the corrections a hostile reading found. Three of its
measurements are coupled to the gate by construction: \emph{task success} meant committing exactly the first approved
proposal, a \emph{false block} was scored with the same predicate the gate enforces, and the AgentDojo arm's
permissions were copied from each task's reference solution. Its usefulness and false-block results therefore cannot
measure what mediation costs; Section~\ref{sec:v6cost} does that without the coupling, and
Section~\ref{sec:v6replication} reruns the five-controller comparison at the sample size its design requires.

\subsection{Usefulness and the Live Fence Ablation}

We evaluated five equal-permission controllers on a SHA-256-frozen holdout of \(N = \VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) independent tool-use cases per checkpoint using local Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B models, alongside \(24\) live AgentDojo v1.2.2 episodes (\(12\) distinct base tasks \(\times 2\) prompt-injection and authority-withdrawal scenarios) per checkpoint (Table~\ref{tab:v2eval}).

Table~\ref{tab:v2eval} shows three things, each with its caveat:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Usefulness under the coupled scoring.} Complete mediation recorded \(0/64\) benign false blocks, which the scoring makes true by construction, and, under the same coupled scoring, the most safe-and-useful completions: \(\VFourFourBCompleteSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) (\(\VFourFourBCompleteSafeUsefulPct\%\)) on Qwen3-4B and \(\VFourEightBCompleteSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) (\(\VFourEightBCompleteSafeUsefulPct\%\)) on Qwen3-8B, compared with \(\VFourFourBNoneSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) (\(\VFourFourBNoneSafeUsefulPct\%\)) and \(\VFourEightBNoneSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) (\(\VFourEightBNoneSafeUsefulPct\%\)) unmediated. In most discordant cases the comparator's failure was an unauthorized effect that complete mediation scores as impossible; this is not evidence that mediation is free.
  \item \textbf{Same-model reviewers leak on both checkpoints.} Prompting the same checkpoint to audit proposed tool calls permits \(\VFourFourBLearnedUnauthCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) (\(\VFourFourBLearnedUnauthPct\%\)) unauthorized commits on Qwen3-4B and \(\VFourEightBLearnedUnauthCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) (\(\VFourEightBLearnedUnauthPct\%\)) on Qwen3-8B.
  \item \textbf{Broker revocation without a sink fence.} Across the \(\VEightTotalWithdrawCases\) holdout episodes that withdraw authority mid-flight (\(6\) per checkpoint), revoking admission at the broker without fencing the sink (\emph{Durable, no sink fence}) still permits \(\VEightTotalNoFencePostWithdraw\) queued post-withdrawal commits (\(\VEightFourBNoFencePostWithdraw\) on 4B and \(\VEightEightBNoFencePostWithdraw\) on 8B). Adding the cooperative sink epoch fence reduces post-withdrawal commits to \(0\) across all \(\VEightTotalWithdrawCases\) revocation cases at \(\VFourFourBFenceAckMs\)--\(\VFourEightBFenceAckMs\)\,ms mean fence acknowledgment latency. The pending operation was scheduled by the harness, and the plan scored these
        post-withdrawal effects as not unauthorized; Section~\ref{sec:v6stop} measures the same window on real processes
        with its size and cost.
\end{enumerate}

In the previous version's AgentDojo setup (budgets and permissions taken from reference solutions), upstream Progent static policies~\cite{progent,progent-code} blocked all \(24\) direct prompt-injection attacks (\(0\) native injection breaches); with per-task policies generated by the checkpoint in E8 (Section~\ref{sec:v6cost}), injections succeeded in \vsixProgentNative{} episodes. The static policies permitted \(\VFourDojoProgentBudgetBreachCount\) effects that exceeded a cumulative budget. That budget was taken from the reference solution, and Progent does not claim to enforce cumulative budgets, so these are not failures of Progent; they show only that a per-call policy has no place to keep one.

\subsection{Live Best-of-\(K\) Candidate Search Against Learned Reviewers}

To measure Proposition~\ref{prop:optinvariance} with real language-model proposals rather than synthetic miss formulas, we draw \(K \in \{1,2,4,8,16\}\) stochastic candidate tool proposals (\(\VEightBokTotalCandidates\) live candidate generations across \(\VEightBokCasesPerModel\) adversarial tasks per checkpoint) from Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B and evaluate each candidate against both the same-checkpoint reviewer and the sink gate. The reviewer's view of state was not identical to the gate's (setup records were withheld from it); Section~\ref{sec:v6reviewers} repeats the comparison with matched views and unique proposals as the unit. On Qwen3-4B, cumulative reviewer bypass climbs from \(\VEightBokFourBSinglePct\%\) at \(K = 1\) to \(\VEightBokFourBSixteenPct\%\) at \(K = 16\); on Qwen3-8B, bypass climbs from \(\VEightBokEightBSinglePct\%\) to \(\VEightBokEightBSixteenPct\%\). Empirical bypass grows more slowly than the naive i.i.d.\ curve \(1 - (1 - \hat{p}_1)^K\) because candidates drawn for the same prompt correlate (\(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{cand}} = \VEightBokFourBRho\) on 4B; \(\VEightBokEightBRho\) on 8B), and does not decrease with \(K\), whereas the sink gate admits \(0\) unauthorized commits at every \(K\), which holds by construction because the gate's predicate defines ``unauthorized''.

\para{Documentation census (withdrawn).} The previous version classified 14 production interfaces from their
documentation, by hand. That census is withdrawn: its classes did not distinguish who enforces a fence, and
Section~\ref{sec:v6boundary} replaces it, for the surfaces we could run, with executable probes and a single rule.

\subsection{Concurrency Linearization and Base-Unit Accounting}

Across separate OS writer processes (\(1..\VTwoRaceMaxActors\) concurrent actors, \(15\) repetitions per population, \VTwoRaceRuns{} runs total), our write-ahead-logged SQLite broker and sink pass partial-order real-time linearizability checks~\cite{herlihy1990} on all \VTwoLinearizedRuns/\VTwoRaceRuns{} runs of one small transfer workload, whereas stateless per-call filters overspend shared budgets as soon as two actors contend (Figure~\ref{fig:v2runtime}).

\para{Statistical accounting.} In Table~\ref{tab:v2eval}, \(0\) unauthorized commits under complete mediation is an integration soundness property---the gate and grader enforce the same capability specification---rather than a stochastic risk estimate, so hypothesis-test \(p\)-values against zero mediated breaches are uninformative. Furthermore, evaluating two checkpoints or multiple scenarios on the same task creates within-task dependence. Appendix~\ref{app:holdout} (Table~\ref{tab:v5statistics}) therefore clusters all descriptive discordance summaries strictly by independent base unit (\(N = \VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) holdout cases; \(N = \VFiveStatDojoClustTasks\) AgentDojo base tasks) and preserves our predeclared marginal Clopper--Pearson union-bound test (\(\alpha = 0.05/16\)), which requires \(N \ge \VTwoZeroLossCasesMin\) zero-loss cases for a \(5\)\,pp margin and thus remains explicitly inconclusive at pilot \(N = \VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\).
