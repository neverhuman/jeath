\section{The Authority Kernel and the Sink Protocol}\label{sec:core}

The broker issues permits; the sink commits effects. Both are Rust processes that keep their state in SQLite (write-ahead
log, \texttt{synchronous=FULL}) and talk over JSON-lines RPC. A permit names one effect, its budget charge, the issuer
whose grant it draws on, the revision it expects and the epoch under which it was issued. The sink commits a permitted
effect only if, at commit time, the charge fits the remaining budget, the issuer's grant is live, the revision matches,
the permit has not been used and its epoch is at least the sink's floor. Raising the floor is the fence. Experiments in
Sections~\ref{sec:v6stop} to~\ref{sec:v6replication} run these processes.

\subsection{What Is Proved}

We verified the kernel of the authority transitions with Verus~\cite{lattuada2023verus,verus}: \VTwoExecFns{} executable
functions and \VTwoTheorems{} proofs discharging \VTwoProofs{} obligations with \VTwoTriggerWarnings{} trigger warnings
(Table~\ref{tab:v2core}), with no \texttt{assume}, \texttt{admit} or external bodies in the verified block. The
properties, each over every input of its type, are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Conservation and post-fence exclusion.} No sequence of operations commits more than the granted budget,
        and after a fence no operation below the new floor commits.
  \item \textbf{Canonical wire bijection.} The fixed-width permit frame encodes and decodes as a bijection and every
        non-canonical frame is rejected, so broker and sink cannot parse one frame two ways.
  \item \textbf{Delegation attenuation.} Along any delegation chain, a child's scope is a subset of its parent's and its
        budget no larger.
  \item \textbf{Relational exclusion.} Two actions declared conflicting (for example, reading a credential and sending
        to the network) cannot both commit within one epoch.
  \item \textbf{Monotone policy ratchet.} A new policy version is accepted only if it permits a subset of what the old
        one did, unless an out-of-band quorum signs the expansion.
  \item \textbf{Skew-buffered quorum quiescence.} A revocation is acknowledged only when every sink shard has signed the
        new epoch or has passed its lease expiry adjusted for a bound \(\delta_{\max}\) on clock skew.
\end{enumerate}

Proofs fail silently when the specification is vacuous, so we pair them with three negative controls. First,
\VFiveProofMutationsRejected{} soundness mutations of the executable code are each rejected by Verus: refunding a
timed-out charge without the sink's authority, bypassing the epoch check, admitting on the lower bound of an uncertain
cost, accepting trailing bytes in the decoder, widening scope on self-upgrade without a quorum, accepting a cluster
acknowledgment from a subset of shards, bypassing the conflict check, committing a staged effect without its verified
postcondition, accepting an unverified widening certificate in the ratchet, and declaring quiescence without the skew
buffer. Second, exhaustive exploration of \CheckedStates{} reachable states (\CheckedEdges{} transitions) of a small
configuration finds no violation under the complete enforcer, while the same exploration with the budget clause
omitted from the specification reaches \OmittedStates{} states and spends 8 units against a 2-unit cap while passing
every remaining check. That is the specification gap of Section~\ref{sec:v6reviewers} in its smallest form. Third,
\VTwoTraces{} traces across all \VTwoAblationSets{} single and paired clause omissions each produce a concrete
violation.

\subsection{Partition Schedules (Simulated)}

A sink shard may be partitioned from the broker when a revocation is sent. Lease expiry can stand in for its missing
acknowledgment only under a timing assumption.

\para{Assumption 1 (bounded clock skew).} Each shard's clock and the broker's differ by at most a known
\(\delta_{\max} \ge 0\).

A shard rejects a permit issued at broker time \(t_{\mathrm{issue}}\) with lease \(L_m\) once its own clock passes
\(t_{\mathrm{issue}} + L_m\). Under Assumption~1, waiting until the broker's clock passes \(t_{\mathrm{issue}} + L_m +
\delta_{\max}\) guarantees this even for a shard whose clock lags by \(\delta_{\max}\); the Verus theorem states this
inequality, and the mutation that drops \(\delta_{\max}\) fails verification.

We exercised the protocol logic in a single-process simulation: three sink threads connected to a coordinator by Unix
socket pairs, under \VFourJepsenSchedulesPerClass{} randomized schedules per protocol class of message drops, delays,
reorderings, duplications, asymmetric partitions, forged acknowledgments and simulated restarts, applied in logical
time. It is in the style of Jepsen~\cite{kingsbury-jepsen} testing but is not Jepsen and does not test a deployed
implementation under real faults; Section~\ref{sec:v6stop} does that for one sink. Each class's outcome follows from its
protocol rule, and the simulation counts how often a randomized schedule exposes it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Signed, skew-buffered fences:} \VFourJepsenCoopLeaks{} of \VFourJepsenSchedulesPerClass{} schedules
        leaked a commit after acknowledgment (\VFourJepsenCoopSignedFences{} acknowledged by signed fences,
        \VFourJepsenCoopLeaseExpiries{} by lease expiry, \VFourJepsenMitmRejected{} forged acknowledgments rejected), and
        \VEightJepsenSkewBufLeaks{} with shard clocks lagging up to 45\,ms under a 50\,ms buffer.
  \item \textbf{Skew buffer omitted:} \VEightJepsenSkewUnbufLeaks{} of \VFourJepsenSchedulesPerClass{} schedules
        (\VEightJepsenSkewUnbufLeakPct\%) leaked.
  \item \textbf{Acknowledged at the broker} while requests were in flight: \VFourJepsenBrokerOnlyLeaks{} schedules
        (\VFourJepsenBrokerOnlyLeakPct\%); \textbf{a sink that ignores epochs:} \VFourJepsenUncoopLeaks{}
        (\VFourJepsenUncoopLeakPct\%).
\end{itemize}
These are counts of schedules with at least one leaked commit, not of leaked commits.

\subsection{Cryptography, Crash Recovery and Confinement}

\para{Cryptography.} The SHA-2, HMAC and Ed25519~\cite{bernstein2012ed25519} code is written from scratch with no
external crates. It enforces canonical scalars and encodings and rejects small-order points, passes the RFC test
vectors and a bidirectional cross-check against the OpenSSL command line, and accepted \VFourCryptoFalseAccepts{} of
\VFourCryptoAttacks{} forgery and malleability attempts. It has not been audited and is part of the trusted computing
base; a deployment should use an audited library.

\para{Crash recovery.} A separate three-process cluster test (SQLite in write-ahead mode with
\texttt{synchronous=NORMAL}, which survives a killed process but may lose the last commits on power loss) drives
\VFiveClusterFuzzFrames{} malformed frames, \VFiveClusterSubagents{} interleaved sub-agent identities with
\VFiveClusterHops-hop delegation from one driver thread, and \VFiveClusterSigkillCycles{} SIGKILL-and-restart cycles
delivered between requests, and reconciles the ledgers with no discrepancy (\(\VFiveClusterMedianUs\,\mu\)s median
commit latency). Kills inside a request are tested in Section~\ref{sec:v6stop}.

\para{Confinement.} Landlock~\cite{landlock} file and network rules and seccomp~\cite{seccomp} filters block the
\VTwoProbes{} direct socket, IPC and resource probes we wrote, so local user-space code must go through the broker
socket. Independent review found two bypasses, a message queue that already existed and an inherited stderr
descriptor; both were fixed and their probes added. The probe set is ours and is not exhaustive.
