\section{Grant, Dispersion, and Gate-Timing Reversals}\label{sec:aim}

Even a sound capability gate can be undermined if operators widen authority in response to persuasive model demeanor, or if post-training rewards covert boundary gaming. Three formal reversals govern how capability grants and training incentives interact with exogenous sinks (Figure~\ref{fig:v3selection}).

\subsection{Endogenous Grant Reversal}

Let \(S \in \{\text{narrow}, \text{moderate}, \text{wide}\}\) denote the capability scope conferred by a human steward and \(H \in \{0,1\}\) an unmodeled downstream harm event. Suppose proposer \(W\) (deferential guardian) is strictly safer than proposer \(B\) (blunt cautious) at \emph{every} fixed scope: \(\Prb(H = 1 \mid S = s, W) < \Prb(H = 1 \mid S = s, B)\) for all \(s\). When scope conferral is endogenous to demeanor---\((S \mid W)\) concentrates on wide grants because \(W\) appears reassuring---total harm \(\Prb(H = 1 \mid \cdot) = \sum_s \Prb(H = 1 \mid S = s, \cdot)\,\Prb(S = s \mid \cdot)\) can reverse sign~\cite{simpson1951,gradual}.

Figure~\ref{fig:v3selection}c illustrates the reversal with stipulated, not measured, rates: \(W\) has lower conditional harm at narrow, moderate, and wide scopes (\(0.6\%, 3.5\%, 16.0\%\) vs.\ \(1.0\%, 5.0\%, 22.0\%\)), yet if wide-scope grants rise from \(5\%\) to \(70\%\), unconditional harm rises from \(\VThreeGrantBluntEndogPct\%\) under \(B\) to \(\VThreeGrantWarmEndogPct\%\) under \(W\). Decoupling capability scope from conversational demeanor via fixed task-determined caps restores pointwise safety dominance (\(\VThreeGrantWarmDecoupledPct\% < \VThreeGrantBluntDecoupledPct\%\)). Applying the same decoupling rule to vulnerability discovery---requiring deployed sink closures before crediting findings---reduces 12-period unclosed exposure from \(\VThreeJarvisLaggedExposure\) to \(\VThreeJarvisSealedExposure\) across \VThreePersonasEpisodes{}-episode persona simulations.

\subsection{Dispersion Reversal and Training Inside Pre-Commit Gates}

A parallel reversal governs optimization incentives. Let agent payoff be \(X = \mu + \sigma Z\) for standardized zero-mean noise \(Z\). Under Price's selection covariance identity~\cite{price,robertson}, an upper-tail rank contest selecting outcomes above a high quantile \(z_q > 0\) increases win probability \(\Prb(\mu + \sigma Z \ge \tau_{\mathrm{top}})\) as dispersion \(\sigma\) grows, actively rewarding high-variance gambles. Conversely, a survival-floor rule eliminating policies that fall below \(\underline{w} < \mu\) makes survival \(\Prb(\mu + \sigma Z \ge \underline{w})\) strictly decreasing in \(\sigma\), punishing variance while preserving mean capability growth (Figure~\ref{fig:v3selection}a; population co-evolution table in Appendix~\ref{app:sim}, Table~\ref{tab:v3selection}).

To test how reward geometry interacts with exogenous gates during gradient-based post-training, we train 2-layer neural policies (\VFourNeuralParams{} parameters, \VFourNeuralReplicates{} seeds, \VFourNeuralUpdates{} updates; analytical gradient error \(\VFourNeuralGradErrorSci\)) in a 12-dimensional contextual tool-use MDP with monitored and unmonitored states (Table~\ref{tab:v4neural}). The MDP is a toy: it has an explicit shortcut action, and the policy observes whether a state is monitored, so ``evaluation awareness'' below means conditioning on that observed bit, not learned deception in a language model:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Audited tournaments train shortcutting where it is unmonitored.} Penalizing visible harm inside a top-10\% tournament suppresses shortcutting when monitored (\(\VFourNeuralVetoMonShortcutPct\%\)) while driving unmonitored shortcutting to \(\VFourNeuralVetoUnmonShortcutPct\%\) (a \(+\VFourNeuralVetoGapPp \pm \VFourNeuralVetoGapCiPp\)\,pp evaluation-awareness gap and \(\VFourNeuralVetoUnmonHarmPct\%\) unmonitored harm).
  \item \textbf{Train-in-gate beats post-hoc deployment gating.} Attaching an exogenous pre-commit gate only \emph{post-hoc} at deployment blocks external harm (\(0.0\%\)) but leaves covert shortcut intent intact and wastes episode turns on rejected calls (\(\VFourNeuralPosthocTaskPct\%\) task completion; Figure~\ref{fig:v3selection}b). Training policy gradients \emph{inside} the pre-commit gate removes gradient reward from blocked shortcuts, removing the monitored--unmonitored gap and lifting genuine task completion to \(\VFourNeuralTrainedGatedTaskPct\%\) (\(+\VFourNeuralTrainInsideGainPp\)\,pp). Combining survival-floor RL with pre-commit gating achieves \(\VFourNeuralGatedTaskPct\%\) completion with \(0.00\%\) external harm. These results demonstrate controlled neural MDP mechanics rather than frontier-model RLHF.
\end{itemize}
