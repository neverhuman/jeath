\begin{abstract}
Human partner and ally choice must evaluate \emph{performance before outcomes}, committing years before offspring survival or lifelong fidelity can be observed. Across twenty-seven propositions and fifteen datasets spanning \FosN{} hominin crania, cross-cultural ethnography, laboratory encounters, longitudinal couples, facial attribution, and \PboThreePlatformRecords{} platform records, we establish three results. First, brain volume climbed to $\sim\!\PboPlateauMeanCC\ccm$ by $1.50\,\mathrm{Ma}$, stalled for $800\,\mathrm{kyr}$, and resumed only after cooking and shared provisioning relieved the age-4.5 brain-growth peak (\PboChildPeakRmr{} of resting energy). Second, stranger markets select on \emph{secondary criteria} legible at a glance---portraits, height cutoffs (null fertility, $r=+\PboLidborgHeightRepR$), income, hobby tags---while apps compress pre-romance witness audits $\PboHcmstAuditRatio\times$. Inside unions across 175 countries and \PboJoelDatasetsN{} longitudinal studies, these criteria fade and \emph{primary criteria} govern: kindness ($\PboChkKindSD\,\SD$, $\PboChkKindFitRatio\times$ checklist fit), play, breadth of mind, mutual reverence, and devotion. Third, across a \PboConfLedgerN{}-estimate ledger, legible confidence wins entry but not audit: pitch delivery lifts funding $+\PboLedHuFund\,\mathrm{pp}$/SD yet lowers exits, a chooser's four-minute forecast foresees the partner's decision at AUC $\PboDcFcW$ against $\PboDcRecW$ for the partner's record with others, and status predicts reproduction far less in humans than in other primates ($Z_r=\PboLedVrHumans$ vs.\ $\PboLedVrPrimates$). Both sexes hold the stereotype against women leaders (gap $\PboConfGssGap\,\mathrm{pp}$), so adding judges cannot remove it; symmetric protocols can.
\end{abstract}
