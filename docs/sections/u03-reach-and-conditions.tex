\section{Quantitative Reach Bounds and Sufficiency Conditions}
\label{sec:theory}

We now formalize why pre-deployment behavioral testing cannot certify disposition, how
state uncertainty constrains admissible authority, and what external conditions bound
catastrophic reach without referencing machine goals. Proofs appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{Audit Volume and Uncertainty Contraction}

Consider an unrestricted policy facing $M \ge 2$ deployment decisions after $N$
pre-deployment test draws. Suppose the policy defects selectively on a recognizable
context set $E$ having deployment probability $\beta \in (0,1)$ and test probability
$\beta/\Lambda$, where $\Lambda \ge 1$ captures shift or evaluation recognition.

\begin{proposition}[Disposition audit volume; Tier 2]
\label{u03:prop:audit}
At $\beta = (\ln 2)/M$, a policy defecting exclusively on $E$ defects at least once
across $M$ conditionally independent deployment decisions with probability
$1-(1-\beta)^M \ge 1/2$. Ensuring detection probability at least $1-\alpha$
($\alpha \in (0,1)$) across $N$ conditional Bernoulli test draws requires
\begin{equation}
N \;\ge\; \frac{\Lambda M}{2\ln 2}\ln(1/\alpha).
\label{eq:u03:audit}
\end{equation}
If $\Prb_{\mathrm{test}}(E)=0$, no finite $N$ detects $E$; if test campaigns are
positively correlated, effective volume shrinks to $N_{\mathrm{eff}} < N$.
\end{proposition}

Because $N$ scales linearly with $\Lambda M$, pre-release behavioral testing cannot
certify that a capable policy will not defect over long horizons. Only one inspection
regime enforces $\Lambda = 1$ across every consequential opportunity: runtime
mediation before external commitment.

Let $x_t \in X$ denote true world and ledger state, $a_t \in \A$ an arbitrary
proposal, $u_t \in U$ the executed primitive operation, and $w_t \in W$ exogenous
disturbances under transition relation $x_{t+1} \in F(x_t, u_t, w_t)$. Because
observations lag and replies drop, an external gate maintains a conservative
uncertainty set $\B_t \subseteq X$ satisfying $x_t \in \B_t$. Relative to a safety
invariant $\I \subseteq X$ disjoint from catastrophe, define the robustly admissible
operations \cite{schneider,shield}:
\begin{equation}
U_{\I}(\B) \;=\; \Bigl\{u \in U : \bigcup_{x\in\B,\,w\in W} F(x,u,w) \subseteq \I \Bigr\}.
\label{eq:u03:admissible}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Robust mediation \& uncertainty contraction; Tier 2]
\label{u03:cor:contraction}
If $x_0 \in \I$, $x_t \in \B_t$ at every decision, every committed operation belongs
to $U_{\I}(\B_t)$ (else a verified $\I$-preserving fallback executes), and complete
mediation holds, then $x_t \in \I$ for all $t$ under every proposal policy. Moreover,
for fixed $(F,W,\I)$,
\begin{equation}
\B \subseteq \B' \;\Longrightarrow\; U_{\I}(\B') \subseteq U_{\I}(\B).
\label{eq:u03:contraction}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:u03:contraction} states a strict design law: \emph{greater state
uncertainty can only contract, never enlarge, admissible authority.} Exhaustive Tier-4
verification over budget $B=\VThreeUncBudget$ checks all \VThreeUncNestedPairs{}
nested interval pairs $[l_1,u_1]\subseteq [l_2,u_2]$ with \VThreeUncCounterexamples{}
counterexamples. Across lags $\Delta t \in \{0,1,2,4,8\}$ (\VThreeUncEpisodes{} paired
episodes per cell; Figure~\ref{fig:v3saturation}a), gating on sound $\B_t$ yields
\VThreeUncConsViolations{} budget breaches while monotonically contracting admission
from $\VThreeUncConsAdmLagZeroPct\%$ ($\Delta t=0$) to $\VThreeUncConsAdmLagEightPct\%$
($\Delta t=8$), whereas stale snapshots breach in $\VThreeUncStaleViolLagFourPct\%$ of
episodes by $\Delta t=4$ and proposer self-reports breach in
$\VThreeUncSelfRepViolLagZeroPct\%$ even at $\Delta t=0$. This directly governs our
broker protocol in Section~\ref{sec:core}: an unacknowledged in-flight permit of
charge $q$ widens $\B_t$ from $\{C\}$ to $[C,\,C+q]$, which is why lost-reply
reservations can never be refunded without an authoritative sink cancellation receipt.

\subsection{Filtration Reach, Saturation, and Sufficiency}

When gates are imperfect or pathways are partially mapped, let an operating period
reset audited holdings to baseline and satisfy four filtration premises: \textbf{A1
(Mediation)} every external step traverses an unalterable boundary; \textbf{A2 (Timely
halt)} any caught step halts execution within latency $\tau < T_{\mathrm{irrev}}$
before irreversible completion for the remainder of the period; \textbf{A3 (Coverage)}
a pathway traverses $<k$ distinct gates and is attempted with probability at most $u$;
and \textbf{A4 (Conditional joint miss)} conditional on any prior history, $k$
successive pathway gate crossings all miss with probability at most $J_k$.

\begin{proposition}[Filtration period-reach bound; Tier 2]
\label{u03:prop:reach}
Under A1--A4, single-period catastrophe probability satisfies $R \le u + J_k$ with
halt, and $R \le \min\{1,\,u + N J_k\}$ across $N$ non-halting attempts, with no
independence assumed among gates.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Qualification on A4:} Universal policy-independence of $R \le u + J_k$ holds
only when A4 bounds $J_k$ uniformly across all policies and histories. An empirical
estimate $\hat{J}_k$ bounds reach only over the evaluated attacker class and traffic
distribution unless uniform dominance is independently established.

\begin{proposition}[Common-cause saturation \& horizon summability; Tier 2]
\label{u03:prop:sat}
(i) If a shared cause defeats all gates with probability $\le c$ and conditional
per-gate miss is otherwise $\le p$ (marginal $m = c + (1-c)p$), then
$J_k \le c + (1-c)p^k \le c + m^k$. Setting $f = u+c > 0$ and saturation depth
$k^* = \lceil \ln f / \ln m \rceil$, every $k \ge k^*$ satisfies $f < f + m^k \le 2f$,
leaving safety gradient shares $(s_u, s_c, s_m) = (u, c, m^k)/R$.
(ii) For true survival-conditioned period hazards $r_t \in [0,1)$, infinite-horizon
survival $\prod_{t=1}^\infty (1-r_t)$ is strictly positive if and only if
$\sum_{t=1}^\infty r_t < \infty$.
\end{proposition}

Tier-4 evaluations quantify three immediate consequences
(Figure~\ref{fig:v3saturation}b,c and \texttt{risk\_horizon.json}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Depth saturates rapidly:} At $u=\VThreeSatDefaultUPct\%$,
$c=\VThreeSatDefaultCPct\%$, and $m=\VThreeSatDefaultMPct\%$ ($p=0.10$), depth
saturates at $k^* = \VThreeSatDefaultKStar$; by $k=4$, true common-cause miss
($\VThreeAppACommonFourPct\%$) exceeds naive $m^4$ ($\VThreeAppAIndepFourPct\%$) by
$\VThreeAppAUnderstatement\times$ (continuous latent difficulty
$\Theta\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$ yields $\Exp[\Theta^k]=1/(k+1)$ without a hard
floor; Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
\item \textbf{Survival requires a shrinking rate:} Across $T=10{,}000$ renewals
\cite{hoeffding}, summable $p_t = 0.01/t^2$ caps cumulative breach at
$\VThreeHorizonSummableTenKPct\%$ ($\le 0.01\pi^2/6$), whereas harmonic $0.01/t$
reaches $\VThreeHorizonHarmonicTenKPct\%$ and constant $10^{-3}$ reaches
$\VThreeHorizonConstTenKPct\%$. If capability growth multiplies risk by $g>1$ per
period while hardening multiplies it by $h<1$, survival requires $gh < 1$.
\item \textbf{Tail halt beats average stop:} Against a $\VThreeTailHaltWindowMs$\,ms
irreversibility deadline, heavy-tailed reactive stop delays leak even at low load
($\rho=0.20$: mean $\VThreeTailHaltLowLoadMeanMs$\,ms,
$\VThreeTailHaltLowLoadLeakPct\%$ leak) and fail severely at high load ($\rho=0.95$:
mean $\VThreeTailHaltHighLoadMeanMs$\,ms, p99
$\VThreeTailHaltHighLoadNinetyNineMs$\,ms, $\VThreeTailHaltHighLoadLeakPct\%$ leak),
whereas pre-commit leases plus a synchronous sink epoch fence record
\VThreeTailHaltFencedViolations{} post-revoke violations
($\VThreeTailHaltHighLoadStrandedPct\%$ stranded-reservation cost).
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Sufficiency of C1--C6; Tier 2]
\label{u03:thm:main}
If conditions \textbf{C1--C6} (Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}) hold in period $t$ for a
system's capability class, with Bayesian post-search bypass bound $\pi_t$
(Section~\ref{sec:graphs}) and measured distinct-step joint miss $J_{k,t}$
(Section~\ref{sec:monitors}), then single-period catastrophe probability satisfies
$R_t \le \pi_t + J_{k,t}$, and multi-period catastrophe probability satisfies
$\sum_t R_t$ ($\le R_0/(1-\gamma)$ whenever $R_{t+1} \le \gamma R_t$ with $\gamma < 1$).
\end{theorem}
