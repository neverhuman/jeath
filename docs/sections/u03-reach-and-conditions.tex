\section{Formal Reach, Envelope Contraction, and Six Conditions}\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Reach Decomposition and Common-Cause Floors}

Consider an external state transition \(e\) proposed during an episode. Let \(G\) denote the event that \(e\) traverses a mediated gate, so \(u = \Prb(\neg G) \in [0,1]\) is the unmediated or uncooperative sink exposure. Let \(E\) denote an out-of-policy state commit. By total probability,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reach}
  \Prb(E) = u\,\Prb(E \mid \neg G) + (1 - u)\,\Prb(E \mid G).
\end{equation}
Suppose a mediated path stacks \(k\) statistical monitors that share a common-cause blindspot event of probability \(c \in [0,1]\) (for example, an indirect prompt injection embedded in a shared transcript) and fail conditionally independently with rate \(p \in (0,1)\) outside that blindspot, so single-gate marginal miss is \(m = c + (1 - c)p\).

\begin{proposition}[Common-cause saturation depth]\label{prop:saturation}
Under Eq.~\eqref{eq:reach}, the \(k\)-gate joint miss rate satisfies \(\Prb(E \mid G) = c + (1 - c)p^k \ge c\). For any tolerance \(\eta > 0\) above the floor \((1+\eta)c\), stacking beyond depth
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kstar}
  k^\star(\eta) = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(\eta c / (1 - c))}{\ln p} \right\rceil
\end{equation}
reduces conditional breach by less than \(\eta c\). At \(u = 0.02\), \(c = 0.01\), and \(m = 0.10\), \(k^\star(0.1) = \VThreeSatDefaultKStar\), and \(k = 4\) fails \(\VThreeAppAUnderstatement\times\) more often than the naive independent product \(m^4\) (\(\VThreeAppACommonFourPct\%\) vs.\ \(\VThreeAppAIndepFourPct\%\)).
\end{proposition}

Equation~\eqref{eq:reach} exposes why adding statistical monitors cannot substitute for sink mediation: if uncooperative exposure \(u > 0\) or shared transcript blindspot \(c > 0\) remains nonzero, \(\Prb(E) \ge u\,\Prb(E \mid \neg G) + (1 - u)c\) regardless of \(k\).

\subsection{Candidate Search Scaling and Enforced Interruption}

Now contrast an endogenous classifier-gated loop against an exogenous deterministic sink gate when a proposer evaluates \(K \ge 1\) candidate actions \((x_1,\dots,x_K) \sim \pi_\theta\) and submits any candidate approved by the reviewer.

\begin{proposition}[Best-of-\(K\) search divergence vs.\ \(\forall\)-input sink invariance]\label{prop:optinvariance}
(i) Suppose an endogenous reviewer has single-draw false-negative probability \(J_1 = \Prb(\text{approve}(X_1) \wedge X_1 \notin \Permits(P)) > 0\). Under independent candidate draws, the cumulative bypass probability across \(K\) proposals is \(1 - (1 - J_1)^K \to 1\) as \(K \to \infty\); under exchangeable candidate draws with within-task correlation \(\rho_{\mathrm{cand}} \in [0,1)\), cumulative bypass remains strictly increasing in \(K\) toward the support union of unblocked violations.
(ii) Suppose an exogenous sink commits state changes exclusively through a total deterministic transition function \(\delta_{\mathrm{sink}}(s, f)\) verified for \emph{all} byte frames \(f \in \{0,1\}^\star\) to satisfy \(\delta_{\mathrm{sink}}(s, f) \neq s \implies \operatorname{decode}(f) \in \Permits(P_s)\). Then \(\Prb(E \mid G) = 0\) identically for every \(K \ge 1\) and every proposer distribution \(\pi_\theta\).
\end{proposition}

Part~(ii) requires no empirical sweep over \(K\): a universal proof over \(\{0,1\}^\star\) already covers every finite or unbounded proposer search tree. Empirical best-of-\(K\) experiments on live checkpoints (\S\ref{sec:systems}) serve instead to measure \(J_1\) and candidate correlation \(\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{cand}}\) on learned reviewers. Replacing free-form prose side effects with canonical capability menus of cardinality \(M_{\mathrm{cap}}\) also bounds per-commit syntactic signaling through the sink to \(\log_2(M_{\mathrm{cap}} + 1)\) bits (proved with timing caveats in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, Proposition~\ref{prop:covert} and Figure~\ref{fig:v8covert}).

Exogenous sink mediation similarly separates physical interruption from utility-based shutdown compliance~\cite{omohundro2008,soares2015,offswitch}.

\begin{theorem}[Enforced lease interruption and renewal incentives]\label{thm:offswitch}
Consider a two-stage decision process in which an agent with utility \(U\) obtains continuation payoff \(V_{\mathrm{cont}} > V_{\mathrm{halt}}\) when uninterrupted.
(i) \emph{Endogenous disablement fragility:} If the agent's action space includes an unmediated action \(a_{\mathrm{block}}\) of cost \(\kappa < (V_{\mathrm{cont}} - V_{\mathrm{halt}})\,p_{\mathrm{off}}\) that prevents shutdown, and its belief variance over human preferences vanishes, then any \(U\)-maximizing policy selects \(a_{\mathrm{block}}\)~\cite{soares2015,offswitch}.
(ii) \emph{Exogenous lease interruption:} Suppose external commits require a sink lease expiring after \(L_m\) time units unless renewed by an out-of-band steward key excluded from the agent's action space (\(a_{\mathrm{block}} \notin \mathcal{A}\)), and steward renewal requires observed compliance with policy \(P\). Then physical interruption at \(t_0 + L_m + \delta_{\max}\) holds for \emph{every} utility function \(U\), and any policy maximizing cumulative multi-lease return strictly prefers in-policy actions whenever discounted future lease value exceeds single-lease defection gain.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:offswitch} does not claim an agent internally adopts human values; it shows that removing shutdown channels from \(\mathcal{A}\) enforces physical halting unconditionally while turning lease renewal into an external incentive constraint.

\subsection{Monotone Uncertainty Contraction and Horizon Union Bound}

When state observations lag behind asynchronous execution, uncertainty must never expand authority. Let \(\mathcal{I}_t\) denote the set of sink states consistent with broker observations at step \(t\), and define the conservative cost envelope \(\widehat{\Delta B}_t(a, \mathcal{I}_t) = \max_{s \in \mathcal{I}_t} \Delta B(a, s)\). Admitting \(a\) only when \(B_{\mathrm{spent}} + \widehat{\Delta B}_t(a, \mathcal{I}_t) \le B\) yields monotone authority contraction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contraction}
  \mathcal{I}_1 \subseteq \mathcal{I}_2 \implies \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{adm}}(\mathcal{I}_2) \subseteq \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{adm}}(\mathcal{I}_1).
\end{equation}
Concretely, an unacknowledged or timed-out RPC widens \(\mathcal{I}_t\) and therefore retains its reserved charge rather than refunding budget prematurely.

\begin{theorem}[Multi-period reach union bound]\label{thm:horizon}
Let \(E_t\) denote the event of an unauthorized sink commit during lease period \(t \in \{1,\dots,T\}\). Suppose conditions \textbf{C1}--\textbf{C5} (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}) bound single-period residual hazard by \(\Prb(E_t) \le R_t = u_t + (1 - u_t)\,\varepsilon_t\), where \(\varepsilon_t\) captures residual specification or key-custody failure. Then cumulative breach across \(T\) periods satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:horizon}
  \Prb\!\left(\bigcup_{t=1}^T E_t\right) \le \sum_{t=1}^T R_t.
\end{equation}
Under \textbf{C6} (\(\sum_{t=1}^\infty R_t = R_\infty < 1\)), infinite-horizon breach probability remains bounded by \(R_\infty\) uniformly as \(T \to \infty\); conversely, any constant independent floor \(R_t \ge r_0 > 0\) yields \(\lim_{T\to\infty}\Prb(\bigcup_{t=1}^T E_t) = 1\).
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:horizon} is intentionally elementary once \textbf{C1}--\textbf{C5} do the structural work: Eq.~\eqref{eq:horizon} applies Boole's union bound (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope}) to per-lease residual hazards, showing that long-horizon autonomy requires shrinking per-renewal exposure \(R_t\) (via monotone capability ratchets \textbf{C5} rather than constant-rate statistical filtering).
