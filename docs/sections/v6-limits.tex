\section{Limitations}\label{sec:v6limits}

\para{What the zeros are.} Five quantities in this paper are fixed by construction, and we report them as such:
\begin{itemize}
  \item complete mediation's unauthorized rate in E1, whose admission predicate is the scoring predicate;
  \item the fence's post-stop commits in E4, since the sink serializes and \texttt{fence} cancels pending records;
  \item duplicate effects, prevented by a schema uniqueness constraint;
  \item the omitted-clause gate's admissions in E5, which equal the single-clause violations of that clause by
        definition;
  \item strict \(S\)'s refusal of AgentDojo's reference attacks in the model-free stage-0 replay, since \(S\) was
        written knowing AgentDojo's public injections.
\end{itemize}
What is measured around each is stated where it appears: the comparators' leakage, the implementation's behaviour
under the faults we injected, the size and cost of the window other layers leave, how often search finds single-clause
violations, and what injections inside \(S\) achieve.

\para{Models and scale.} All live results use local, quantized, open-weight checkpoints (\vsixCheckpointList) on CPU.
Agents and reviewers decode greedily; only E5's proposer samples (temperature 0.8). We ran no frontier model, by budget
decision. A greedy review of a repeated proposal repeats its verdict, so we count distinct proposals and take cases as
the unit. On the larger checkpoint, a reviewer that reasoned at length already closed the gap
Section~\ref{sec:v6reviewers} reports on its small cases; we did not test larger states, other reviewers, or control
protocols that resample, defer or audit.

\para{Benchmark coverage.} E8 covers two of AgentDojo's four suites and one attack template, through this repository's
JSON tool loop rather than native function calling. Progent's policies come from the same open-weight checkpoint (its
paper used GPT-4o), with adaptive policy updates disabled. E8b's injections and graders are ours and were chosen to lie
inside \(S\); they show the blind spot exists but do not estimate how often attackers find it. Across checkpoints (E2)
the comparison is descriptive: chat template, protocol compliance and Progent policy quality all vary with the
checkpoint.

\para{What the sink is.} The SQLite sink mediates only its own database effects. Nothing here is a sandbox,
external exactly-once execution, or a formal refinement of a deployed system. The fenceability probes use single-node
reference instances in default configuration, and two surfaces come from documentation only.

\para{Verification boundary.} The mechanized kernel proves properties of its own state machine and of the permit
encoding. In the release binaries used by the experiments, the broker and sink decisions on the v2 path are made by
unverified Rust that calls verified leaf predicates; the full kernel comparison runs only in debug builds. The
cryptographic primitives are a from-scratch implementation that has not been audited.

\para{Preregistration.} Every v6 plan was committed to this repository's history before the data it governs, and amendments record what was seen
before them. Three launches were stopped and rerun (Appendix~\ref{app:v6ledger}): E1 for a proposal-expiry defect, E8
for arm-dependent denial text, and an E5 smoke run for a change of strategy. None of the stopping decisions used an
outcome aggregate. However, the commits are on no public remote, and several hand-typed timestamps needed errata, so
the timing cannot be verified externally. E6's plan was finalized after its two development runs and is labelled
accordingly; E6b's plan precedes its data.

\para{Validators.} The validators recompute every reported number from raw artefacts (sink databases, episode logs,
model outputs), but they are not independent implementations. The E8 validators import the harness's own
specification and AgentDojo's graders. The E5 validator mirrors the harness's clause logic, and its independent check is
agreement with the Rust gate. E4's timing is re-derived from the harness's own log. A reader should treat them as replay
checks, not second opinions.

\para{No end-to-end control evaluation.} Each experiment tests one component: the stop layers with a synthetic agent,
reviewers on fixed cases, a specification on a benchmark, fences on single-node surfaces. None puts an adaptive red
team against the whole system, a monitor, a stop and a fenced sink together, as a control evaluation~\cite{control}
would. That evaluation would show whether the components compose, and it is the most important piece this paper lacks.

\para{Conferral.} The independence of reach from the proposer holds at a fixed grant. Whether more capable agents earn
broader grants, and whether that reverses the comparison (Observation~\ref{obs:conferral}), needs a study with people,
which this version does not contain.
