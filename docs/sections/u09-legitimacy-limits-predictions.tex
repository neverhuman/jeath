\section{Enforceability, Scope Limits, and Tested Predictions}\label{sec:limits}

\subsection{Enforceability, Adequacy, and Legitimacy}

Exogenous sink mediation solves only one of three distinct governance problems, and conflating them overstates what any technical mechanism can achieve. \emph{Enforceability} asks whether declared capability bounds hold despite adversarial optimization, concurrency, and asynchronous revocation; our Verus proofs and cooperative sink fences address enforceability. \emph{Specification adequacy} asks whether the declared capability policy captures the real-world hazards of the sociotechnical system~\cite{leveson2011,normal}; no formal proof prevents harm omitted from the specification. \emph{Institutional legitimacy} asks who holds steward keys, how capability budgets are set, and what accountability governs emergency revocation~\cite{ostrom1990,iasr}. Exogenous sinks make institutional rules physically binding on tool execution, but they do not write the rules or legitimate the stewards.

\subsection{Boundary Failure Modes}

Four explicit boundaries limit our guarantees:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{In-policy destructive actions.} If a capability grant authorizes editing a repository file or spending \(2\) budget units, a harmful edit or bad transfer within that envelope commits cleanly (Table~\ref{tab:v2core}).
  \item \textbf{Uncooperative external sinks.} Interfaces lacking idempotency keys, conditional-write epochs, or pre-dispatch hold buffers (Section~\ref{sec:v6boundary}) enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:reach} as positive unmediated exposure \(u > 0\).
  \item \textbf{Lower-layer TCB and clock-skew assumptions.} Verification covers our Rust state machine and wire codec, not the Linux kernel, SQLite storage engine, physical side channels~\cite{kocher1996}, or hardware clock faults exceeding \(\delta_{\max}\).
  \item \textbf{Pilot live sample size and prospective human/frontier scope.} Predeclared marginal union bounds on the pilot's frozen \(N = \VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) checkpoint holdout were under-powered relative to \(N \ge \VTwoZeroLossCasesMin\); E1 reruns that comparison at \(N = 128\) (Section~\ref{sec:v6replication}). Frontier-scale RLHF and human delegation experiments remain prospective (Appendix~\ref{app:holdout}).
\end{enumerate}
