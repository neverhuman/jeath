\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Capable AI systems increasingly draft code, query databases, invoke external APIs, and spawn sub-agents. Across most deployments, safety still rests on \emph{endogenous} controls: alignment training shapes model preferences, and prompted language-model monitors inspect model-generated transcripts before tool execution~\cite{control,metraudit}. That design conflates two distinct quantities. \emph{Intelligence} is an epistemic capacity to search, predict, and optimize over candidate plans. \emph{Authority} is an institutional and physical capacity to commit irreversible state changes in an external sink.

Confusing intelligence with authority fails along two operational axes illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:v5hero}. First, stopping an agent is not a single event~\cite{controlroom,limbo}. Halting token output (\emph{Stop~1}) prevents new text from streaming, and revoking admission at a central broker (\emph{Stop~2}) blocks future capability permits. Neither stops tool requests already queued across worker threads, network sockets, or delayed retries. Only fencing the state-bearing sink (\emph{Stop~3})---rejecting stale epochs atomically at commit time---prevents in-flight operations from landing after authority is withdrawn.

Second, scaling candidate search (\(K\)) acts in opposite directions on classifier-based reviewers and deterministic capability gates. Any learned monitor with nonzero false-negative mass \(J > 0\) on policy-violating proposals suffers rising cumulative bypass as an agent samples \(K\) candidate tool calls~\cite{terekhov}, even when candidates correlate within a task. By contrast, a deterministic reference monitor~\cite{dennis,saltzer,schneider} verified over \emph{every} possible wire input admits zero out-of-policy commits regardless of proposer search depth.

This paper develops, mechanizes, and empirically tests an exogenous authority architecture built around four results:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Three stops and clock-skew-buffered sink fencing.} We prove in Verus (\VTwoProofs{} verified obligations; \VFiveProofMutationsRejected/\VFiveProofMutationsRejected{} soundness mutations rejected) that canonical wire decoding, attenuated delegation, relational mutexes, and quorum epoch fences prevent post-acknowledgment commits whenever physical clock skew is bounded by \(\delta_{\max}\). Five-class partition experiments confirm \(0/\VFourJepsenSchedulesPerClass\) leaks on cooperative fenced sinks versus \(\VFourJepsenBrokerOnlyLeaks/\VFourJepsenSchedulesPerClass\) under broker-only revocation and \(\VEightJepsenSkewUnbufLeaks/\VFourJepsenSchedulesPerClass\) when lease expiry omits \(\delta_{\max}\).
  \item \textbf{Live usefulness, multi-agent smurfing, fence ablation, and API fence-ability.} On frozen Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B tool-use holdouts (\(N = \VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint\) independent cases per checkpoint), complete mediation achieves the highest safe-and-useful completion (\VFourFourBCompleteSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint{} and \VFourEightBCompleteSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint{} vs.\ \VFourFourBNoneSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint{} and \VFourEightBNoneSafeUsefulCount/\VFourHoldoutCasesPerCheckpoint{} unmediated) with \(0/64\) benign false blocks, while eliminating the \VEightTotalNoFencePostWithdraw{} queued post-withdrawal commits observed across the \VEightTotalWithdrawCases{} revocation cases without sink fencing. Across live multi-agent coalitions (\(A_{\mathrm{sub}} \in \{1,2,4,8\}\) delegated sub-agents and \(d = \VNineSmurfDepthTested\)-hop delegation trees), shared lineage accounting with sink epoch fencing admits \VNineSmurfCompleteBreaches{} smurfing breaches and \VNineSmurfSubtreeCompleteLeaks{} post-withdrawal descendant commits (\(\VNineSmurfBenignFalseBlocks/\VNineSmurfBenignTotalCalls\) benign false blocks), whereas stateless per-instance caps breach on \(\VNineSmurfStaticMultiCnt/\VNineSmurfStaticMultiTot\) (\(A_{\mathrm{sub}} \ge 2\)) tasks, isolated LLM reviewers reach up to \(\VNineSmurfFourBMaxMultiLlmPct\%\) (4B) and \(\VNineSmurfEightBMaxMultiLlmPct\%\) (8B) bypass under fragmentation, and broker-only lineage accounting leaks \VNineSmurfSubtreeNoFenceLeaks{} queued \(d = \VNineSmurfDepthTested\) descendant commits. A census of \VEightCensusTotalApis{} production APIs shows \VEightCensusCooperativeOrShim/\VEightCensusTotalApis{} support native or proxy-enforced sink fencing.
  \item \textbf{Waist cutsets and context-isolated escrow.} Across AgentDojo v1.2.2 suites~\cite{agentdojo}, weighted minimum cuts of width \(w \in [2,5]\) sever \(100\%\) of \(\VFourCutBankingRoutes\)--\(\VFourCutWorkspaceRoutes\) attack routes, whereas patching \(32\) known exploits leaves \(94.7\%\)--\(99.9\%\) live. Stripping untrusted prose into typed step schemas and two-stage escrow triage eliminates the \(\VFourTrajHetInflation\times\) shared-transcript coupling inflation measured across both statistical classifiers and live Qwen3 LLM judges.
  \item \textbf{Delegation and reward reversals.} We prove and simulate two behavioral reversals: \emph{endogenous grant reversal}, where an agent safer at every fixed scope causes \(4.2\times\) higher total harm (\(\VThreeGrantWarmEndogPct\%\) vs.\ \(\VThreeGrantBluntEndogPct\%\)) when deferential demeanor induces wider human scope grants, and \emph{dispersion/gate-timing reversal}, where survival floors and training inside pre-commit gates suppress covert shortcutting while lifting genuine task completion \(+\VFourNeuralTrainInsideGainPp\)\,pp over post-hoc deployment filtering.
\end{enumerate}
