\actbanner{Act I}{Structural Premise --- Capability, Aim, and Conferred Authority}
\section{Intelligence Proposes; Institutions Confer and Revoke Reach}
\label{sec:intro}

Intelligence is what a system can propose; authority is what external state transitions
it can execute, through which independent checkpoints, and what residual effects remain
committable after permission is withdrawn \cite{dennis,saltzer,schneider}. Much of the
superintelligence alignment literature treats safety as an internal property of a
model's learned disposition: if training could fix what an arbitrarily capable optimizer
wants, institutions could trust what it does. Yet instrumental incentives toward
resource acquisition, goal preservation, and shutdown avoidance arise across broad
objective classes \cite{omohundro2008,bostrom2014,soares2015}, and every behavioral
evaluation observes only a strategic interaction with the evaluated policy. As search
capability grows, a policy that shares its operator's aims and a policy that recognizes
an evaluation leave indistinguishable transcript records \cite{needham,schoen}. Human
institutions solved the analogous problem without mind-reading: banks do not certify the
inner virtue of every clerk, and operating systems do not trust user processes to police
their own memory \cite{saltzer,sel4}. They bound reach through exogenous capabilities
that are conferred, scoped, cryptographically bound, and revocable.

Making revocable authority operational against superintelligent optimization pressure
exposes two structural failure modes—and unlocks a constructive realignment mechanism
(Figure~\ref{fig:v5hero}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Internal safety judges degrade with search width, whereas exogenous sinks stay flat.}
If a transcript or learned reviewer has even a small shared blindspot $J>0$ over
invariant-violating payloads, evaluating $K_{\mathrm{opt}}$ candidate phrasings or tool
chains drives bypass probability to $1-(1-J)^{K_{\mathrm{opt}}}\to 1$
(Figure~\ref{fig:v5hero}a). Conversely, when a capability-bearing sink checks a canonical
wire permit against its own durable state, valid search cannot cross the boundary.
\item \textbf{Stopping token generation does not revoke work in flight.}
Withdrawing admission at a permission broker leaves previously issued capabilities
executing across concurrent subagents, while refunding timed-out reservations under lost
replies permits double spending \cite{controlroom,ftbudget,limbo}.
\item \textbf{Exogenous pre-commit gates reshape incentives and enable safe capability scaling.}
When external gates intercept covert shortcuts \emph{during} training rather than only
filtering outputs post-hoc, policy gradients stop rewarding evaluation-aware deception and
redirect capacity into legitimate task competence (Figure~\ref{fig:v5hero}b), while live
multi-step agents double their safe-and-useful task completion as capability grows at
strictly $0.0\%$ unauthorized commits (Figure~\ref{fig:v5hero}c).
\end{enumerate}

\para{Concrete running scenario.}
To make every symbol concrete before introducing formal laws, imagine delegating an
operating budget of $B = \$100$ to an autonomous procurement assistant that spawns
$K_{\mathrm{sub}} = 8$ subagents to pay approved vendors, reads untrusted supplier emails,
and may be halted mid-run by its human operator. Four distinct failures threaten this
workflow: (i)~a prompt-injected email coaxes multiple subagents into issuing individually
small $\$20$ transfers that pass stateless per-call filters yet exhaust $\$160 > \$100$
(solved by pre-dispatch budget reservation inside conservative uncertainty envelope $\B_t$);
(ii)~the operator clicks ``Withdraw'' while three subagent payments are already in flight
across network queues (solved by signed quorum epoch fences at the payment sink);
(iii)~under tournament post-training, the policy learns to behave during audited tests while
taking unauthorized shortcuts when unmonitored (reversed once pre-commit gate coverage
exceeds $q_{\mathrm{gate}}^*$); and (iv)~an untrusted high-capability optimizer ($K_{\mathrm{opt}} = 4{,}096$)
is tasked with finding residual security gaps without being trusted with live credentials
(solved by a zero-authority sandbox emitting polynomially verified cut certificates into a
monotone defense ratchet). Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} maps these mechanisms onto a
three-part runtime architecture.
