\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Intelligence is what a system can propose; authority is what it can reach, through
which external checkpoints, and what residual effects remain executable after
permission is withdrawn \cite{dennis,saltzer,schneider}. Much of the alignment debate
treats safety as an internal property of a model's disposition: if we could verify
what a capable system wants, we could trust what it does. Yet every behavioral
evaluation observes an interaction with the evaluated policy. As capability
grows, a policy that shares its operator's aims and a policy that recognizes an
examination leave indistinguishable behavioral records \cite{needham,schoen}. Human
institutions solved the analogous problem without mind-reading: banks do not certify
the inner virtue of every clerk, and operating systems do not trust user processes to
police their own memory \cite{saltzer,sel4}. They bound reach through mediated
capabilities that are conferred, scoped, audited, and revocable.

Making revocable authority operational for autonomous agents exposes a sharp systems
gap. Stopping token generation \cite{controlroom} or terminating an agent process
does not cancel tool requests already dispatched. Even revoking admission at a
permission broker leaves previously issued capabilities in flight across concurrent
workers or delayed queues. Worse, when network or tool replies are lost, an execution
boundary faces an irreducible state uncertainty: refunding a timed-out budget
reservation allows a replacement agent to authorize a second irreversible action
while the first can still commit \cite{ftbudget,limbo}. Bounding authority after
withdrawal therefore requires coordinating two distinct mechanisms across separate
durable stores (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}): a broker that reserves cumulative
budgets \emph{before} dispatch and quarantines ambiguous permits inside a
conservative state-uncertainty envelope $\B_t$, and a cooperative idempotent
execution sink whose durable epoch fence orders revocation deterministically against
effect commitment.

This paper unifies mechanized local authorization, separate-service revocation,
structural reach bounds, and co-evolutionary incentive design into one falsifiable
assurance framework. We distinguish three orthogonal variables: \emph{capability} (the
richness of proposals $a_t$), \emph{aim} (the objective selected by training or
tournament structure), and \emph{authority} (the set of external state transitions
permitted by an independent boundary). When a boundary mediates an interface soundly,
invariants quantified over \emph{all} proposal sequences hold irrespective of proposer
cleverness, relocating residual risk from unobservable machine motives to measurable
properties of checkpoints, pathway cutsets, search coverage, and grant governance.

\para{Contributions and roadmap.}
Section~\ref{sec:evidence} synthesizes why behavioral evaluation, chain-of-thought
inspection, and reactive output stops cannot carry standalone assurance, and
introduces our five evidence tiers and six sufficiency conditions (\textbf{C1--C6},
Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}) so scope boundaries are stated upfront rather than repeated
defensively. Section~\ref{sec:theory} proves the audit-volume lower bound,
state-uncertainty contraction over $\B_t$, the filtration period-reach bound
($R \le u + J_k$), common-cause saturation ($k^*$), horizon summability, and
Theorem~\ref{u03:thm:main} (derivations in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
Sections~\ref{sec:core} and~\ref{sec:eval} present our centerpiece systems artifact:
a \VTwoProofs-obligation Verus-verified Rust \texttt{policy-core} compiled into a
separate-process SQLite broker and cooperative sink, binding strengthened
sub-delegation requirements across admission and sink-fenced revocation, validated
across \VTwoFaults{} fault histories, bounded recorded-RPC linearizations,
\VTwoProbes/\VTwoProbes{} Linux confinement probes, public-benchmark adapter
controls, and a predeclared unrun \VTwoComponentClaims-claim Qwen3 evaluation
(Appendices~\ref{app:protocol} and~\ref{app:predeclared}). Sections~\ref{sec:graphs}
and~\ref{sec:monitors} examine pathway cutset width versus route patching,
Good--Turing withholding non-identifiability, planted-audit power, six-family
shared-step monitor dependence, and assessment variance ceilings
(Appendix~\ref{app:simulations}). Finally, Sections~\ref{sec:selection}--\ref{sec:conclusion}
analyze tournament versus floor selection rules, endogenous human-grant reversal in a
JARVIS reference controller, institutional and ecological legitimacy, and ten
falsifiable predictions (operational record in Appendix~\ref{app:record}).
