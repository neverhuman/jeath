\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
AI control protocols act on proposals: a monitor reads what an agent proposes, and an agent that is caught is stopped.
Stopping the model does not stop the tool calls it has already made. Counted from the decision to stop, about
\(\lambda(L+w)\) of them still commit, for call rate \(\lambda\), enforcement delay \(L\) and tool latency \(w\). A
control evaluation that scores a caught attack as prevented does not count them; we do not measure how many
would be harmful. Halting inference, or revoking permits at the broker that issues them, leaves both terms. Fencing
the sink that commits the effects, so that it refuses work issued before the stop, removes \(\lambda w\); only faster
enforcement removes \(\lambda L\). The arithmetic is Little's law and the fence is a fencing token; we measure both
where AI control stops agents. On real broker and sink processes with a synthetic agent, a median of
\vsixPhaseHaltMedian{} effects committed after a halt took effect, \vsixPhaseRevokeMedian{} after a revocation and
\vsixPhaseFenceRange{} after a fence (by design), which discarded \vsixPhaseFenceLostMedian{} legitimate calls. A gate
that reads state at commit catches by construction what a reviewer of proposals can miss, and reviewers missed it:
shown the rules and the state, a \vsixEFiveCkpt{} reviewer giving a bare verdict accepted an approved proposal that
the state had since made unauthorized in \vsixReviewerExact{} of \vsixExactCases{} cases (\vsixEFiveCROneSecondHost{}
on another CPU); with reasoning it accepted \vsixReasonerExact{}, and \vsixEFiveCTwinsPhrase{} where the state permitted
them. But a gate admits whatever its specification permits. On AgentDojo's \vsixEEightTasks{}, under a specification
written per suite, a \vsixEEightCkpt{} agent completed \vsixStrictUtility{} benign tasks (\vsixUnprotectedUtility{}
unprotected, \vsixProgentUtility{} under Progent), and injections written to fit the specification succeeded in
\vsixStrictInside{} episodes (\vsixUnprotectedInside{} unprotected, \vsixProgentInside{} under Progent). Of
\vsixFenceMechanisms{} fencing mechanisms we probed, only \vsixFenceAllThree{} was enforced by the surface, refused a
forged epoch and held in all \vsixFenceAllThreeRaces{} races. All models are small open-weight checkpoints on CPU.
