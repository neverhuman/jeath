\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
A model's ability to propose an action is not the right to commit it. We hold that right as state outside the model and
check it where effects commit. An operator can stop an agent in three places:
halting inference, revoking admission at a broker, or fencing the sink that commits effects. On real broker and sink
processes with a \vsixStopLatency\,s mean tool latency, \vsixHaltRange{} effects committed after a halt took
effect, \vsixRevokeRange{} after a revocation, and \vsixFenceRange{} after a fence, which discarded \vsixFenceLost{}
legitimate calls in flight. A same-model reviewer admitted \vsixReviewerState{} unauthorized proposals whose violation
lies in state (a replayed budget, a withdrawn issuer, a stale revision) and \vsixReviewerVisible{} whose violation is
visible in the proposal. A gate that reads state has no such blind spot by construction. Its blind spot is what its
specification omits: on AgentDojo, with grants written without the benchmark's solutions, injections that stayed
inside the grant succeeded in \vsixStrictInside{} episodes, and benign utility was \vsixStrictUtility{} with
mediation and \vsixUnprotectedUtility{} without. Mediation thus turns a learned blind spot into a written
specification gap. Every guarantee holds only at sinks that can refuse a stale epoch, for local open-weight models,
and at a fixed grant.
