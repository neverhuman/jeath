\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
AI control protocols act on proposals: a monitor reads what an agent proposes, and an agent that is caught is stopped.
Calls approved before the catch may still be running, and stopping the model does not stop them. Counted from the
decision to stop, about \(\lambda(L+w)\) effects still commit, for call rate \(\lambda\), enforcement delay \(L\) and
tool latency \(w\); a control evaluation that counts a caught attack as prevented does not see them. Halting inference
or revoking admission at a broker leaves both terms. Fencing the sink, so that it refuses at commit any work issued
before the stop, removes \(\lambda w\), the larger term when long work ends in a short commit (a merge after a test
run, a capture after a payment is authorized); only faster enforcement removes \(\lambda L\). On real broker and sink
processes, with a synthetic agent whose calls were all authorized when issued and the stop enforced at once, a median
of \vsixPhaseHaltMedian{} effects committed after a halt took effect,
\vsixPhaseRevokeMedian{} after a revocation and \vsixPhaseFenceRange{} after a fence (by design), which discarded
\vsixPhaseFenceLostMedian{} legitimate calls. A gate that reads state at commit also catches what a reviewer of
proposals can miss: shown the rules and the state, a \vsixEFiveCkpt{} reviewer giving a bare verdict accepted, in all
\vsixExactCases{} cases (\vsixEFiveCROneSecondHost{} on a second CPU), an approved proposal that the state had since
made unauthorized; with reasoning (median \vsixReasonerTokens{} tokens) it accepted \vsixReasonerExact{}, and in a post
hoc control \vsixEFiveCTwinsPhrase{} where the state permitted them; a per-rule verdict without reasoning
(median \vsixEFiveDTokens{} tokens, post hoc) closed the gap on \vsixEFiveDClosed. But a gate admits whatever its specification
permits. On AgentDojo, under a specification written per suite without the reference solutions, a \vsixEEightCkpt{}
agent completed \vsixStrictUtility{} benign tasks (\vsixUnprotectedUtility{} unprotected, \vsixProgentUtility{} under
Progent), and injections written to stay inside the specification succeeded in \vsixStrictInside{} episodes
(\vsixUnprotectedInside{} unprotected). Of \vsixFenceMechanisms{} fencing mechanisms on PostgreSQL, etcd and a
filesystem, only \vsixFenceAllThree{} was enforced by the surface, refused a forged epoch and held while stale writers
ran. All models are small open-weight checkpoints run locally on CPU.
