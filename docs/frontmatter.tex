\textbf{Abstract.}
A more capable AI need not receive more authority, and a caring objective does not itself justify that authority. We separate incentives, permitted effects, legitimate authorization, specification adequacy, and enforcement. A policy-independent invariant can survive a proposer upgrade within its fixed transition model; empirical estimates require renewed justification under changed opponents and exposure. A fresh Rust reconstruction exhaustively checks \CheckedStates{} states and \CheckedEdges{} state--action pairs while admitting all 20 specified useful tasks. Nine enforcement mutations admit counterexamples. Omitting the aggregate-budget predicate passes \OmittedEdges{} checks while permitting four times the intended spend. A SQLite prototype additionally exercises real concurrent processes, exact-payload approvals, required steward grants, revocation, renewal, retries, and process death before and after commit. Its protected effects remain inside one database. Newly trained small classifiers test shared-input dependence, independently sampled inputs, and feedback-guided attack selection; graph, reporting-coverage, and reward-selection fixtures expose further assumptions. These are bounded engineering and synthetic results, not frontier-model safety estimates. Care-oriented training and human delegation remain untested hypotheses. The contribution is an executable assurance program with preserved sources, observable effects, counterexamples, and explicit limits; it does not establish safe superintelligence.
