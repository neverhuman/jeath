\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
Many AI control protocols act on proposals: a monitor reads what an agent proposes, and an agent that is caught is
stopped. Stopping the model does not stop the tool calls it already made. Counted from the decision to stop, about
\(\lambda(L+w)\) of them still commit, for call rate \(\lambda\), enforcement delay \(L\) and tool latency \(w\). A
control evaluation that scores a caught attack as prevented does not count them; we do not measure how many would be
harmful. Halting inference, or revoking permits at the issuing broker, leaves both terms. Fencing the sink that
commits effects, so it refuses work issued before the stop, removes \(\lambda w\); only faster enforcement removes
\(\lambda L\). This is Little's law and a fencing token, measured where AI control stops agents. On real broker and
sink processes with a synthetic agent, enforcing at once, a median of \vsixPhaseHaltMedian{} effects committed after a
halt took effect, \vsixPhaseRevokeMedian{} after a revocation and \vsixPhaseFenceRange{} after a fence (by design),
which refused \vsixPhaseFenceLostMedian{} legitimate calls in flight. A gate that reads state at commit
catches by construction what a proposal reviewer can miss, and one without reasoning did: shown the
rules and the state, a \vsixEFiveCkpt{} reviewer giving a bare verdict accepted an approved proposal that the state had
since made unauthorized in \vsixReviewerExact{} of \vsixExactCases{} cases (\vsixEFiveCROneSecondHost{} on a second
CPU); with reasoning it accepted \vsixReasonerExact{}, yet accepted \vsixEFiveCTwinsPhrase{} the state permitted. A
gate moves the failure into its specification, which has costs and gaps. On \vsixEEightTasks{} of AgentDojo, under a
specification written per suite, a \vsixEEightCkpt{} agent completed \vsixStrictUtility{} benign tasks
(\vsixUnprotectedUtility{} unprotected, \vsixProgentUtility{} under Progent), and injections that fit the
specification succeeded in \vsixStrictInside{} episodes (\vsixUnprotectedInside{} unprotected, \vsixProgentInside{}
under Progent). Of \vsixFenceMechanisms{} fencing mechanisms we probed, only \vsixFenceAllThree{} was enforced by the
store itself, refused a forged epoch and held in all \vsixFenceAllThreeRaces{} races. All models are small open-weight
checkpoints on CPU.
