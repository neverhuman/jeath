\textbf{Abstract.}\enspace
AI control protocols act on proposals: a monitor reads what an agent proposes, and an agent that is caught is stopped.
Stopping the model does not stop the effects it has already dispatched. We hold an agent's authority as state, checked
where effects commit, which separates three ways to stop an agent: halt inference, revoke admission at a broker, or
fence the sink so that it refuses work issued before the stop. On real broker and sink processes, effects that commit
after a stop decision grow as \(\lambda(L+w)\), for call rate \(\lambda\), enforcement delay \(L\) and tool latency
\(w\). The fence removes the \(\lambda w\) term (\vsixHaltRange{} effects committed after a halt took effect,
\vsixRevokeRange{} after a revocation and, by design, \vsixFenceRange{} after a fence, which discarded \vsixFenceLost{}
legitimate calls); only faster enforcement removes \(\lambda L\). A same-model reviewer shown the rules and the state
admitted \vsixReviewerState{} unauthorized proposals whose violation lay in state and \vsixReviewerVisible{} whose
violation was visible; reasoning at length (a median of \vsixReasonerTokens{} tokens per review instead of
\vsixReviewerTokens) brought the first to \vsixReasonerState. A gate is blind instead to what its specification omits.
On AgentDojo, a specification written without the benchmark's solutions stopped its injections (\vsixStrictNative,
against \vsixUnprotectedNative{} unprotected) but completed \vsixStrictUtility{} benign tasks against
\vsixUnprotectedUtility, and injections written to stay inside it succeeded in \vsixStrictInside. A fence needs a sink
that can refuse a stale epoch: of \vsixFenceMechanisms{} mechanisms on PostgreSQL, etcd and a filesystem, only
\vsixFenceAllThree{} was enforced by the surface, refused a forged epoch and let no stale write through. All models are
small open-weight checkpoints run locally.
