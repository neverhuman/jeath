\section{Feedback on every draft}

\subsection*{The early drafts}
\lab{V3 (\TotVthree).} A selection-index treatment with many formal statements and no measurement. It establishes the
identity the later drafts rest on and nothing about people. \emph{To improve:} everything the later drafts did.

\lab{CF, Consolidated Final (\TotCF).} The strictest about what may be inferred, with two counterexamples worth keeping
(better prediction of a phenotype can mean weaker selection on its heritable part; a product of correlations does not fix
the sign of a missing link). It says almost nothing about what women and men select for, who decides, or what any of it
means now, which is 34 points of this rubric. \emph{Kept:} its cautions, as objections.

\lab{FT, Final (\TotFT).} The founding narrative told well: hunting, contests, play and games as early evidence. No model,
no data, 63 references, several claims stated as fact. \emph{Kept:} the narrative, and its instinct that play and games
matter, which returns in V9 as the result on safe contests.

\lab{PB, Publication (\TotPB).} A cautious integrative review with plots of published values on the first page. The
AI-generated illustration beside data would not survive review. \emph{Kept:} the first-page figure as a requirement, and the
division of partner value into capacity and willingness.

\subsection*{The long lineage, V4 to V8}
\lab{V4 (\TotVfour).} Ten propositions with proofs, a reanalysis of 233 crania, verified references. Nothing on living
people. \lab{V5 (\TotVfive).} Adds 34{,}092 survey respondents and splits selection into access and number: the first result
about women and men that a reader can check. \lab{V6 (\TotVsix).} A tighter article, an independent audit, and three episodes of
selection. \lab{V7 (\TotVseven).} What a test can show, enforcement, and the pairing gate by cohort; two results taken from the
companion paper on AI oversight. Common weaknesses of all four: no data on what people choose, one paragraph on culture,
the word ``choice'' used as if the bride chose, and a first page that told half the story.

\lab{V8 (\TotVeight).} Adds the jury law measured on 8{,}378 speed dates, men's misreading of interest, a seven-step table of
the sexes, a culture table of nine published comparisons, and a section for builders of AI. \emph{Weaknesses the review
notes exposed:} it never said who the chooser was or what coercion does to the argument; it called two small-brained
species ``beside'' large-brained ones; it left the ``half of men'' claim, the shrinking brain, storytelling and the
present-day stakes unaddressed; and its conclusions were a list of findings, not a statement of what follows.

\subsection*{The short lineage, S2, F2 and VF}
\lab{S2, Final Synthesis (2) (\TotStwo).} Excellent discipline and prose, and four corrections that V8 adopted. No analysis of
its own. \lab{F2, FINAL (2) (\TotFtwo).} The timing problem as the opening and the jury law as a measurement: the best new
idea of any draft before V8. Overreaches in four places listed in the previous scorecard; no supplement; no culture.

\lab{VF, v8\_FINAL (\TotVF).} The strongest draft I did not write. \emph{Title:} ``It takes a village to read a mind, and
that is how we got ours'' is the most quotable line in the project; its second half asserts a cause the paper does not
establish. \emph{New knowledge:} coded ethnography from three samples (who chooses a spouse, payments, the age gap,
polygyny, grounds for divorce), a variance decomposition of speed-dating decisions, eight results. I recomputed its
headline ethnographic numbers independently and they agree (8\% of daughters and 31\% of sons choose alone; forager men
marry 6.7 years later). \emph{Mate selection:} the best account of who decides, under the heading ``A jury decides, and
more firmly for daughters'', and of divorce as continued assessment. \emph{What it does not do:} it reports consent codes
but not rape, capture or wife-beating, and has no account of what coercion does to the mechanism; it has no cross-national
data on stated ideals or pairing; ``selection was never the bottleneck, so brain size tracked a moving optimum'' and
``exactly as the Spearman--Brown law predicts'' still overreach; its survey $N$ differs from the audited one by twelve
respondents; and neither code nor figure sources were supplied, so nothing in it can be rebuilt. \emph{Read and layout:} the
best first page of any draft before V9, five numbered steps each with a one-line gloss. \emph{Kept in V9:} the tagline's first
half, the jury finding (re-derived from the raw codes, with coercion added), the age gap and its relation to polygyny, and
divorce as exit.

\subsection*{V9, the final paper (\TotVnine, self-assessed)}
\emph{What it adds to V8.} Three new data sets (the coded ethnographic record; 14{,}399 people in 45 countries; 117{,}293
in 175), a second extract of the national survey, two propositions and two corollaries with proofs and simulation checks,
four audit checks, three figures and a sixth panel on the first page, six predictions, seven objections, 56 further
verified references, eight stated conclusions, and a section of the supplement that answers each review note in turn.

\emph{Where it is still weak.} Each data result still rests on one sample of its kind. The ethnographic codes are outsiders'
judgments of recent societies. The cross-national files are convenience samples of self-report. The fall in births is
located, not explained. The main text has grown to fifteen pages and the supplement to forty-five; a journal version would
cut both by half. Nothing estimates an ancestral quantity, and the paper says so on the first page.

\section{Answers to the review notes}

{\small
\begin{longtable}{@{}P{0.20\textwidth}P{0.42\textwidth}P{0.31\textwidth}@{}}
\toprule
Note & What the evidence says & Verdict, and what changed in V9\\
\midrule
\endhead
\lab{1. The opening assumes consent. Was rape common?} & In 186 coded societies a daughter chooses her husband alone in 8\%; parents choose in 45\%, and in 33 societies she cannot easily object. Her consent is necessary in a third of 57 societies. Rape is reported in 50 of 95 societies and rated common in 13 of 31; women were taken captive in war in 37\% of 158; wife-beating is present in 80\% of 70. Divorce is open equally to both in 77\% of 93. No source measures the share of conceptions that followed coercion. & \emph{Right about the word; the mechanism survives.} It needs a jury with years of evidence, and the record names parents and elders. New equation: coercion scales the pathway by one minus its share and selects the body instead. New main-text section 2.9, Fig.~7, Table~S29, objections O45--O46, prediction P30.\\
\lab{2. Only half of men reproduced; twice as much female DNA.} & The source is real: mitochondrial lineages coalesce about twice as deep as Y lineages (Wilder et al.\ 2004). It measures a ratio of effective numbers. New result: for either sex, effective number over census number equals $(1-q)/(1+\alpha)$, where $q$ is the childless share and $\alpha$ the inequality among parents. A twofold ratio fits 80\% and 40\%, and equally fits the same share of each sex with unequal fathers, or clan-level competition with no difference at all (Zeng et al.\ 2018). In the United States the ratio is 1.01, rising to 1.14 in the latest cohort. & \emph{Half right.} Men's reproduction was the more unequal; the headcount cannot be read from the ratio; and every child gets half its other chromosomes from each parent, so ``twice as much female DNA'' is false as stated. I could not verify the attribution to Scott Galloway; the paper cites the earlier popular source. Proposition~18, Fig.~7D.\\
\lab{3. Was \textit{Homo floresiensis} beside australopiths, or isolated?} & Isolated. Flores, reached only by sea; remains about 100,000--60,000 years old; ancestors there by 0.7 million years ago. The last australopiths lived in Africa a million years earlier. Its brain is of australopith size, which is why it sits low on the plot. & \emph{The note is right; V8's wording (``beside'') invited the error.} Text corrected in two places, and isolation is now part of the explanation.\\
\lab{4. ADHD as an advantage for hunters; a 20\% figure.} & Verified: a \textit{DRD4} variant goes with better nourishment in nomadic Ariaal men and worse in settled ones (65 and 87 men); it shows past positive selection and is commoner in far-migrating populations; adults who screen positive leave depleted patches sooner and earn more in a foraging task. Against: alleles associated with the diagnosis have declined since the Paleolithic, long before farming. & \emph{Plausible for exploration traits; not shown for the clinical condition. The 20\% figure is not in the source and could not be traced.} New corollary: a mixed profile is neutral under broad comparison and a deficit under a narrow test. Prediction P33.\\
\lab{5. Brains shrank; cognitive alleles rose about one SD; more with less.} & Both hold with caveats: over 5\% less brain mass in 50,000 years, largely with body size, with the last 3,000 years disputed; and a one-SD rise over 10,000 years in West Eurasia in alleles that predict cognitive measures today (verified in the abstract). & \emph{Compatible, and what the tissue-cost proposition predicts.} The comparison with distilled models is kept as an analogy and added to the AI section.\\
\lab{6. Neurodivergence selected for the forest; sharper senses.} & Foragers name odors far better than neighboring farmers who speak a closely related language, which points to practice. No study found of heritable sensory acuity favored in foragers and relaxed in farmers. & \emph{Not supported as stated.} One sentence, and a caution against projecting diagnoses onto the past (O50).\\
\lab{7. Storytellers have more children; networks; fire; the book \emph{Outbound}.} & Skilled Agta storytellers have more surviving children; better-connected Agta and BaYaka mothers have more living offspring; firelit talk is stories (Wiessner 2014); people without electricity fall asleep 3.3 hours after sunset. I could not identify a book titled \emph{Outbound} on this subject. & \emph{Supported.} Fire enters the model twice beyond diet: more occasions and observers, and speech as a domain everyone is compared on. New paragraph in 2.3; prediction P31.\\
\lab{8. Maasai: lion hunting and raiding replaced by jumping; jump height decides standing and marriage.} & The tournament is real (conceived 2008, first held 2012; six events, the high jump modeled on the warriors' dance) and was created to replace the lion hunt as the mark of manhood. Marriage by custom follows the ceremony that ends warriorhood. No source makes jump height a condition of marriage. & \emph{Supported as substitution; the marriage claim is not.} Standing is what the jump confers.\\
\lab{9. Safe, repeatable mating rituals.} & Games with winners are recorded in 460 of 486 societies. Sereer wrestlers have more children, though women say they do not value wrestling. & \emph{Supported, and now derived:} enough repetitions of a safe trial match one dangerous one for capacity, and never for nerve. Corollary~9, Eq.~4, prediction P32.\\
\lab{10. Specialization, online personas, loss of community, falling births.} & In the United States the ideal number of children is unchanged since 1972 (2.5). Children per adult aged 25--39 fell from 1.70 to 1.26, and 94\% of the fall is accounted for by more people never marrying. Monthly evenings with neighbors fell from 60\% to 35\% of young adults. Against: seeing friends goes with being single; an hour of social media a day is unrelated to being single in 70 countries; couples who met online married sooner in one panel. & \emph{Supported as accounting and trend; the mechanism is conjecture.} New section 2.11, Fig.~9, Proposition~19, predictions P34--P35. On generalists: division of labor removes the premium on general ability; nothing here shows they survive better.\\
\lab{The conclusions are not strong or important enough.} & & \emph{Accepted.} Section 3.1 now states eight conclusions with the status of each, and the first page carries the present-day one.\\

\section{The fifty suggested sources}

I went through the list. Two were downloaded and analyzed, and are in the paper with a figure and a table: the
175-country survey (Kowal et al.\ 2025; CC~BY~4.0) and the 45-country survey (Walter et al.\ 2020). They add the finding
that both sexes everywhere say they want kindness and intelligence above looks and money; that women want resources more
than men in all 70 countries with enough data while the size varies forty-fold; and a replication of the door outside the
United States. Two papers from the list were added as evidence (Hunt et al.\ 2015 on length of acquaintance; Stinson et
al.\ 2022 on romance that begins as friendship). The Columbia file and the General Social Survey were already in use.

Not used, and why. The eight public-domain Dryad deposits would have been useful (the face-rating study in five countries
is a second test of agreement among observers), but Dryad refused file downloads from this workspace; attach the files and
I will add them. \emph{How Couples Meet and Stay Together}, the Northwestern speed-dating data and the European panels need a
signed agreement, which is yours to give. The National Survey of Family Growth is public and is the right next data set
for the births result. The online-dating summaries I tried were not openly readable.

\section{Title, subtitle, authors}

The title stays, in two colors. The tagline is the first half of VF's subtitle, which is the best line anyone has written
for this project: \emph{It takes a village to read a mind.} The subtitle follows: \emph{How being judged at everything, by
people who knew us for years, could build a mind for anything, and what happens when the jury goes home.} The last clause
is new and carries the present-day conclusion. ``Could'' stays.

Erin Beilharz is second author. Author biographies with photographs follow the references, as in a journal. Her

\section{What a leading journal would still ask for}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \lab{A specialist as author or reviewer.} No human expert in evolutionary anthropology or demography has read it.
Journal policies on AI-assisted manuscripts must be checked.
\item \lab{Second samples.} Register data from another country for the episodes; a speed-dating sample that is not graduate
students; fertility histories with partnership dates for the births result.
\item \lab{One direct test.} P27 (longer meetings, more observers) or P34 (shared acquaintances and time to union) can each
be preregistered.
\item \lab{Full-text verification} of every source, and a specialist's reading of the ethnographic codes.
\item \lab{A shorter paper.} The article should be cut from fifteen pages to eight for a general journal. Three results
would lead: the jury law, who decided, and where the births went.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Checks run on the final paper}

All 435 references cited are in the bibliography, and the 410 that have a DOI resolve against Crossref. Every number that
comes from an analysis is written into the text by script from the results files. The audit passes: exact enumeration for
the effective-number result (difference $10^{-16}$); the three parts of the births decomposition sum to the change; two
ethnographic variables recounted from the raw file by a second reader (difference zero); the count of countries in which
women want resources more than men recomputed with a different reader (difference zero). Seventeen of the nineteen results
files carried over from V8 are identical, and the other two only gained entries. The first survey extract is unchanged
byte for byte. VF's headline ethnographic numbers were reproduced independently. The single-file source compiles on its
own to the same 62 pages. The first page, every new figure and the pages with new tables were inspected as images.

\textbf{Rebuild from scratch.} The package was unpacked into an empty folder with every result, figure and generated
input deleted; all five public data sources were fetched again and verified (the survey, the speed-dating file, the
ethnographic codes at the pinned commit, and the two cross-national files by digest); and the whole pipeline was rerun
(nineteen minutes). All 23 results files and every generated number and table came out identical, and the rebuilt PDF
matches the released one word for word, bibliography included. Wording edits made to two source files after the rerun began
were compiled in both copies before the comparison.
